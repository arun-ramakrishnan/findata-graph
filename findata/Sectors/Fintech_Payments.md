## Newsletter synthesis — Fintech_Payments (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across payments/lending (PayTM, Mobikwik), merchant/POS (Pine Labs), insurance aggregator (PB Fintech/PolicyBazaar), broking app (Groww), and credit card issuer (SBI Cards). Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions.

**UPI is the universal on-ramp; monetization is the asymmetric bet:** UPI volume is structural and cross-cutting — Mobikwik's UPI share of GMV jumped from 32% (Q3 FY25) to 41% (Q3 FY26) on record GMV for 12 straight quarters, and SBI Cards' RuPay UPI spends grew >20% QoQ in Q3 FY26 led by Tier-2+ users. But MDR/monetization remains the contested variable: PayTM (Deora) and Mobikwik (Bipin Preet Singh) both flag MDR-on-UPI/PPI as "already in motion from RBI" and a coming "new source of revenue," while PayTM has structurally pivoted away from free QR deployment toward monetizable Soundbox devices to decouple from subsidy dependence (PIDF).

**Lending tail is being re-underwritten around proprietary data, not traffic-led distribution:** PayTM (Sharma) explicitly contrasts "payment-led credit" vs "traffic-led distribution" and is restarting Postpaid/BNPL with faster traction (1 lakh+ customers in 3 months; ₹100cr disbursal target in 6 months). Mobikwik (Taku) confirms credit distribution "picking up again — up about 30-32% in Q4 and Q1" after the post-Diwali unsecured-lending squeeze, and launched 'First Card' (RuPay-on-UPI) for first-time credit access. Pine Labs (Rau) notes rivals pivoting *toward* lending just as PayTM pivots *toward* data-led lending — implying sector-wide repositioning around the credit tail.

**Tier-2/3 India is the consensus growth geography across payments, broking, and credit:** Groww (Keshre) reports new tier-2/3 customers are "more long-term oriented... building wealth over time, not just trading"; SBI Cards flags Tier-2+ as the fastest-growing RuPay UPI segment; Mobikwik (Taku) positions itself as "a full-stack financial platform for Bharat — Tier-2 and Tier-3 India"; PayTM's merchant loan runway (7%→20% penetration) is implicitly a Tier-2/3 merchant-credit story. This is the single most consistent demographic theme.

**AI is no longer a buzzword — it's a stated productivity and margin lever, with measurable outputs:** Pine Labs (Rau) quantifies it: "89% of all new code written within Pine Labs over the last two quarters has been completely AI-generated." Groww (Keshre) calls 2026 "the inflection point for how AI will start impacting things" on both CX and internal velocity. PB Fintech (Sarbvir Singh) expects AI to materially impact customer service, renewals, and claim support over 1-3 years while sales stays human-led. Mobikwik (Komal Sharan) is using AI to "reimagine a lot of customer journeys" to keep indirect costs at ₹110cr or lower. PayTM (Sharma) is building proprietary AI models "to lead the race of AI in this country."

**Distribution economics are consolidating toward scale + claims/service quality, not subsidies:** PB Fintech/PolicyBazaar (Dahiya) anchors the thesis: "people will buy insurance from the person who can help them at the point of claims" — 3-4 years of claims-support investment is now driving retention, renewals, and PR. Pine Labs (Rau) sees newcomers "pulling out of the hardware-heavy model as it isn't economically viable." PayTM (Sharma) is "removing the deadwood continuously" and exited credit-card distribution because traffic alone isn't a moat. The sector-wide signal: subsidy/traffic-led models are being pruned in favor of service-led retention.

**Regulatory architecture (RBI PA/PG, MGA, expense caps, DLG) is reshaping business models rather than gating growth:** Mobikwik (Taku) confirms newly acquired PA/PG and stock-broking licenses as the foundation for a "full-stack" pivot. Mobikwik (Komal Sharan) explains DLG-model adoption is *compressing* near-term lending margins (cost front-ended, revenue back-ended) — a regulatory accounting effect. PB Fintech (Dahiya) frames expense-cap regulation as enabling ("within or 35%, please go ahead") and flags MGA as "the single most transformational move that can happen in the Insurance industry." PayTM (Deora) is recalibrating around the loss of PIDF subsidy. *[Inference]*: regulation is redistributing economics across the stack rather than capping aggregate sector growth.

**International/adjacency expansion is the second arc for scaled players:** Pine Labs (Rau) is exporting the Indian tech stack to Philippines, Vietnam, UAE ("producing dollar-based revenues") and launching stablecoin/programmable-money settlement for global markets. PB Fintech (Dahiya) has spent 3-4 years scanning Middle East, SE Asia, Europe for acquisitions; UAE insurance premiums grew 76% YoY and "turned profitable kind of forever." PayTM (Sharma) is exploring "adjacency software to other fintechs in neighboring geographies." *[Inference]*: India-built fintech stacks are being productized as exports, though India remains the volume engine.

> "QR only merchants are negligible. I mean, my sales team does not even deploy QR merchants. Internal funnel for device deployment is, churn the other competition's device. It is not QR. We used to do it. And we used to deploy QR and upgrade them to Soundbox. Now we're like, okay, the bad device is out there. Let's churn them into us."
> — Vijay Shekhar Sharma, Founder and CEO, One 97 Communications (PayTM)

> "MDR on PPI, UPI, something that is already in motion from RBI and it is under discussion in the payments ecosystem... From our perspective, it will definitely bring like new source of revenue... a decent double-digit percentage of our GMV will benefit from MDR."
> — Bipin Preet Singh, CEO, One Mobikwik Systems

> "89% of all new code written within Pine Labs over the last two quarters has been completely AI-generated. It does two things for us: one, it provides tremendous efficiency as we build new products, and two, our legacy platforms are being updated and improved as we speak."
> — Amreesh Rau, Chief Executive Officer, Pine Labs

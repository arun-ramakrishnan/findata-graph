## Newsletter synthesis — Housing_Finance (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across HFCs (Aavas, Can Fin, Aadhar, Aptus, Bajaj Housing, HomeFirst, plus Plotlines' sector overview covering LIC Housing, PNB Housing, GIC Housing, Repco). Sources span The Chatter, Plotlines and Points & Figures editions.

**Disbursement-recognition policy is the new governance fault line:** Aavas has moved to a *realization-based* model (cash-on-disbursement, not sanction), trading optical growth for fidelity and explicitly re-basing Street expectations to ~18–20% — while Can Fin is taking the opposite purity route, refusing co-lending and construction finance outright and insisting "DSAs are only sales enablers; the core work will be done by our organization." Both are betting that *underwriting ownership* (not headline disbursal velocity) is the durable moat.

**Cost of funds is the active NIM lever — every HFC is pre-positioning the liability book for the rate-cut cycle:** Aavas has shifted 61% of borrowings to EBLR/short-tenor MCLR so liabilities reprice *faster than assets*; Can Fin moved all bank borrowings from MCLR to repo-linked two years ago and is now raising term loans at 7.10% top-end; HomeFirst reports sub-8% marginal cost of borrowing (COB) in Jun–Jul with AA rating momentum; Aadhar runs ~73% floating borrowings vs ~75% floating assets for symmetrical transmission. *[Inference]*: spread protection in FY26 comes from liability-side choreography, not lending-rate hikes.

**Ticket size is being pushed up deliberately — affordable HFCs are walking away from the smallest loans:** Aptus raised its minimum login ticket to >₹7L in Q2 FY26 and plans ~₹1L/year inflationary step-ups (₹10L today → ₹12L in two years), explicitly "to move to a better category, better class of customers" — *not* because of small-ticket delinquency. Bajaj Housing's near-prime/affordable SBU runs ₹40–60L in top-36 markets and only drops to ₹15–35L in tier-4/rural. HomeFirst's co-lending push is designed to "raise average ticket size while yield holds ~13%+." *[Inference]*: the affordable label is migrating toward lower-middle, not true low-income.

**Asset quality has quietly normalized to near-zero incremental provisioning — but only for the in-house, salaried-skewing players:** Can Fin's Suresh Iyer disclosed "only for the growth we've had to provide for the provisioning of INR 3 crores… in terms of the delinquent accounts, there is almost 0 kind of provisioning"; Aavas retains strong-credit customers and lets weaker ones attrite ("Wherever we let go, the performance really deteriorates"). Plotlines flags Can Fin's stable GNPA / ~15 bps credit cost as the "purity play" dividend. *[Inference]*: clean asset quality is the *payoff* of refusing co-lending/CF and owning origination, not a sector-wide windfall.

**Co-lending is splitting the sector into two camps — institutionalizers vs refusers:** HomeFirst is scaling co-lending toward ~10% of disbursements (₹434cr book, +87.5% YoY) as a structural second rail "without compressing origination yields"; Can Fin is categorical: "We will not be doing co-lending with banks and we will not be doing construction finance." Bajaj Housing is leaning on its AAA cost-of-funds argument to compete directly in prime rather than cede economics to a partner. *[Inference]*: co-lending is no longer experimental — it's a strategic fork.

**Geographic and segment mix is being rebarbelling — Urban/Emerging, North/South, salaried/SENP all at once:** Aadhar has formalized a two-speed "Urban vs Emerging" GTM (130 branches in top-15 cities; Emerging at 45% of business, targeting 50:50); Can Fin is rotating from 89%→85% housing (target 80% in three years) and shifting toward SENP and North/West; Aptus already sources 21% of Q1 FY26 originations through referral/construction-ecosystem/social apps in Tier-3/4. *[Inference]*: HFCs are no longer "pure-play" anything — the spread is in segment specialization.

**HFCs are losing system share to banks but the leaders are outgrowing the HFC pack — attrition, not origination, is the binding constraint:** Per Bajaj Housing's data, HFC home-loan share fell from 21.3% (FY20) to 18.3% (FY25) while BHFL itself grew 22.1% CAGR FY23–25 vs 8.8% for the HFC cohort. Atul Jain flags disbursement attrition rising from 15–16% to "upwards of 20–22%… mostly going to PSU banks" as the *single* reason for cutting medium-term AUM guidance, with stabilization only expected 3–4 months after the next rate cut. *[Inference]*: the bank-vs-HFC war is being fought on the *existing-book* side, not new disbursals.

**Regulatory tailwinds (GST 2.0, e-Khata reforms) are now cited as demand catalysts, not just compliance overhead:** Aadhar's Rishi Anand calls GST 2.0 cuts on cement/marble/granite/bricks a material affordability lever for low/middle-income housing; Can Fin credits Karnataka's B-Khata→A-Khata and 15-day e-Khata windows for the recovery from ₹78cr to ₹270cr monthly disbursements. *[Inference]*: state-level property-registration plumbing is a bigger near-term disbursement driver than repo cuts for affordable/tier-2 players.

> "We have undertaken a historic step to move to a realization-based model for disbursement recognition. This strengthens governance, aligns revenue recognition with actual cash flows, and reduces volatility from sanction-to-disbursement timing."
> — Sachinder Bhinder, MD & CEO, Aavas Financiers

> "We will not be doing co-lending with banks and we will not be doing construction finance… DSAs are only sales enablers; the core work will be done by our organization. We're adding our own sales team… and plan 15 new branches in H1."
> — Suresh Iyer, MD & CEO, Can Fin Homes

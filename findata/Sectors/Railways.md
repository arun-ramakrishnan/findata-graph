## Newsletter synthesis — Railways (multi-edition)

*Cross-cutting themes distilled from company concalls and newsletter coverage. Every theme is supported by ≥2 companies. Quotes are verbatim from named sources. Inferences are labelled.*

### THEMES

- **Capex pipeline at multi-year highs, but bidding intensity is compressing margins:** Railway capex has scaled to ~₹2.65 lakh crore (FY25 budget, ~2× FY20's ₹1.48 lakh cr) and Oriental's note flags a record ₹2.9 lakh cr FY27 outlay — a multi-year demand engine cited by RVNL, Titagarh, RITES, IRCON, Dhara Rail and Oriental. The flip side, voiced most bluntly by IRCON (up to 24 bidders per railway tender) and RITES (competitive orders now 63% of order book, 70%+ of fresh inflow), is that the same capex is attracting intense competition, dragging EPC margins down 0.5–1% and pulling consultancy yields lower on competitively-won work.

- **Order books are deep, young and executable — but conversion takes time:** RVNL guides a ~50/50 revenue mix over three years (~₹40,000 cr railway nomination + diversified bidding across Vande Bharat, BharatNet, highways), RITES sits on ₹9,400 cr with >50% just 12–18 months old, Railtel holds ₹10,166 cr (43% state governments, 25% Indian Railways), and Dhara Rail's book is anchored in Indian Railways/RVNL/IRCON packages. The common refrain — explicit at IRCON ("even after picking up orders, the execution and the conversion into revenue takes some time") — is that revenue visibility is high but FY26 turnover will be broadly flat as the book converts.

- **EPC vs item-rate / nomination mix is shifting toward competitive bidding:** RITES discloses the clearest mix transition (competitive orders 63% of book vs historically nomination-led), and IRCON's CMD frames the same shift as a margin trade-off ("we will not be bidding under losses"). RVNL is deliberately diversifying away from pure railway nomination toward Vande Bharat, BharatNet, highways and international work. *Inference:* the sector-wide drift from item-rate/nomination to lump-sum/EPC competitive bids is structural, not cyclical, and is the single biggest swing factor for blended margins.

- **KAVACH + signalling/telecom is the breakout modernisation vertical:** IRCON has won the first KAVACH tower tender (₹194 cr, Central Railway) plus a ₹253 cr South Western Railway KAVACH order and is building a signalling-diagnostics vertical; Railtel deploys KAVACH alongside VSS at 4,652 stations and Wi-Fi at 6,117; and Quadrant Future Tek (Points & Figures) holds an ₹8,287 mn KAVACH order book with 15,512 km already equipped and full-network rollout targeted in 6–8 years. Indian Railways' ~₹7,500 cr signalling-telecom allocation and ~₹1.2 lakh cr safety outlay (FY26-27) underpin a multi-year tech upgrade cycle distinct from civil EPC.

- **Rolling stock: passenger/metro mix rising over freight wagons, Vande Bharat is the anchor tender:** Titagarh's CFO expects passenger rail revenue to equal/exceed freight within three years (Bangalore/Kolkata metro anchors) and is shifting from metal fabrication to a "technology/engineering firm" to qualify for high-speed rail; RVNL flags Vande Bharat prototype delivery in June/July and a ~7% mechanical (Vande Bharat) share of its order book; Oriental cites 144+ Vande Bharat trains operational (400+ targeted by 2030) and ~1,000 new trains planned over five years. The passenger pivot is the most consistent strategic message across private wagon/coach OEMs.

- **Electrification is essentially complete — the opportunity pivots to upgrade, O&M and 2×25 kV:** Per Points & Figures (Cues in the Clutter), Indian Railways is ~99.08% electrified (69,315/69,961 RKM), 24 states/UTs fully electrified, with only 646 RKM remaining in FY26. Jayant Infratech's note frames the post-electrification opportunity as 2×25 kV upgrades, 5–10-year OHE/substation O&M contracts, private DFC/metro/logistics electrification, and solar/KAVACH integration. Per the same edition, Indian Railways spends ~₹160 billion/year on diesel and can save ₹80–100 billion annually by shifting to electric traction (~50% cheaper than diesel); Net Zero 2030 commitments fund the next leg. *Inference:* pure greenfield electrification EPC (Jayant's historical sweet spot) is a shrinking TAM; the growth pool is upgrade + O&M + high-voltage tech work.

- **Supply-chain bottleneck (wheel sets) is resolving, unlocking wagon run-rates and backward integration:** Titagarh confirmed the multi-quarter wheel-set shortage resolved from August 2025, restoring an 800–850 wagons/month run rate (against 1,000 capacity) and Q1 FY27 in-house aluminium coach production. Jupiter Wagons is investing ₹3,500 cr in a greenfield Odisha wheel & axle forging facility (100,000 forged wheel sets/yr; axle lines mid-FY27, wheels early FY28) to structurally address the same risk. Oriental is partnering with United Wagon Company/VNICT on next-gen 25-ton axle load gondola wagons. The wheel-set chokepoint — a sector-wide constraint — is converting from a margin headwind into a backward-integration moat.

- **Wagon leasing / annuity models emerging as a margin-stabiliser (inference):** *Inference — no direct IRFC commentary was found in the corpus; the theme is inferred from Oriental's strategic pivot.* Oriental is moving into wagon leasing (recurring-revenue, smart-wagon + KAVACH-integrated) to dampen manufacturing tender cyclicality, and Titagarh reports 20–30% EBITDA service-contract margins emerging from supply-plus-servicing models. The directional signal — manufacturers reaching for leasing/annuity economics — is consistent across both private OEMs even though IRFC's lessor-perspective commentary on tenor/yield was not located in the scanned editions.

### QUOTES

> "Around 24 bids are coming in railway tenders, per tender. 17 number of bidders are there in road projects tenders, and 19 number bidders are there in building bids. So, it's a tough competition."
> — Harimohan Gupta, Chairman & Managing Director, IRCON International

> "In the next three years, we expect the revenue contribution from the passenger rail segment to equal or exceed that of our freight segment. This shift is intentional as passenger contracts typically offer higher visibility and better margins. Our recent investments in the Bangalore and Kolkata metro projects are proof of this transition."
> — Anil Kumar Agarwal, Chief Financial Officer, Titagarh Rail Systems

> "As these competitive orders start generating revenue — if you compare the mix of the order book at the end of the financial year on March 31, 63% is competitive. If you count the fresh order inflow, it is about 70%+. The margins across all our streams on the new orders are much lower."
> — Rahul Mithal, Chairman and Managing Director, RITES

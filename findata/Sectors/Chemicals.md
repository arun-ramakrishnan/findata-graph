## Sector Overview

The Chemicals sector in India encompasses specialty chemicals, agrochemicals, petrochemicals, industrial chemicals, and performance materials. India is a global powerhouse in chemical manufacturing with competitive cost advantages and growing export orientation.

## Sub-Sectors

- **Specialty Chemicals** - High-value chemicals for specific applications (see also Specialty Chemicals sub-sector)
- **Agrochemicals** - Pesticides, fertilizers, and crop protection chemicals
- **Petrochemicals** - Derivatives of petroleum and natural gas
- **Industrial Chemicals** - Bulk chemicals for manufacturing
- **Performance Materials** - Advanced materials for specialized uses

## Key Characteristics

- **Market Size**: ₹8+ lakh crore chemical industry
- **Growth Drivers**: Import substitution, export growth, manufacturing expansion
- **Regulatory Environment**: REACH, BIS standards, environmental compliance
- **Key Challenges**: Raw material volatility, environmental regulations, global competition

- **Value Addition**: Shift towards specialty and high-value chemicals

- **Regulatory Risk**: Environmental compliance and safety standards

## Newsletter synthesis — Chemicals (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across specialty chemicals, agrochemicals, plastic additives and battery-material precursors. Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions covering SRF, Deepak Nitrite, EPL, Rain Industries, Neogen Chemicals, Ester Industries, NOCIL, Anupam Rasayan, Pidilite, BASF India, Himadri Speciality Chemical, Gujarat Fluorochemicals, Tatva Chintan, Premier Explosives and others.

**China dumping is the dominant overhang, but correction is viewed as a matter of "when," not "if"** — aggressive, below-cost Chinese pricing is pressuring margins across specialty chemicals (SRF, BASF India), sodium nitrite/DASDA/nitro-aromatics (Deepak Nitrite), and plastic/rubber additives (NOCIL, BASF). Management uniformly frames this as unsustainable. SRF's Ashish Bharat Ram calls it "not viable in the long run," while NOCIL and Deepak Nitrite are leaning on anti-dumping petitions (NOCIL's DGTR decision is a near-term catalyst) and BIS mandates as more durable structural shields than tariffs.

**The agrochemical destocking cycle is bottoming and turning, but recovery is staggered across the chain** — channel destocking is largely complete per UPL ("structural reset underway") and Sharda Cropchem ("global inventory levels are now normal"). Volume revival is showing up — Anupam Rasayan's CEO confirms "volumes are kind of coming back"; Deepak Nitrite expects Q4 FY26 improvement from near-zero agrochemical volumes; SRF sees "signs of revival" in crop-protection. Recovery is uneven: PI Industries gives conservative mid-single-digit guidance despite 31% new-product growth, indicating base-business headwinds persist.

**"China Plus One" is translating into concrete export order gains and capacity repositioning for India** — NOCIL cites "US tariffs on China redirecting volumes via Europe, positioning NOCIL as a China-plus-one supplier"; Rallis India is "building an export customer base to benefit long-term from the China Plus One strategy"; Coromandel shifted NPK share to 60% (from 51%) specifically to replace "DAP shortfall from lower China supplies." Rain Industries' Gerard Sweeney notes China's smelting caps mean "the supply response to this sustained demand growth will increasingly come from smelters outside China."

**A defensive pivot toward pharma intermediates / CDMO is underway to escape agrochemical cyclicality** — SRF is adding a second pharma-intermediate plant (₹180 cr) to "reduce dependence on the volatile agricultural market," targeting a doubling of pharma's specialty-chemicals contribution from ~10% to 20%. Anupam Rasayan's Jayhawk acquisition builds "a global integration specialty and CDMO platform," explicitly positioning to capitalize on the US Biosecure Act to "lead supply chains away from restricted geographies." Sharda Cropchem and PI Industries are likewise extending into pharma/CRDMO.

**The capex cycle is bifurcating — big bets on battery materials and new energy, but discipline and pauses elsewhere** — Gujarat Fluorochemicals is committing ₹1,600 cr FY26 capex (₹1,200 cr for EV battery materials) in a "business-model pivot"; Himadri has ~₹1,770 cr lined up across LFP greenfield and battery-material integration; Deepak Nitrite is spending ₹2,000 cr on nitration/hydrogenation/fluorination. In contrast, Rain Industries has "paused Telangana brownfield expansion due to weak demand — no expenditure incurred," signaling capex is being gated by demand visibility.

**Green chemistry, circular economy, and plastic recycling are moving from ESG narrative to revenue strategy** — Ester Industries ties its growth thesis to Loop's recycled rDMT/rMEG, citing EU virgin-plastic taxes, UK recycled-content tax, and India's 10–30% recycled-packaging norms as demand accelerants. Himadri is developing LiB recycling ("lowers CO₂ vs virgin materials by 70–90%"). Rain Industries is expanding into graphite recycling from spent lithium-ion batteries as a "circular economy play." Lead Reclaim and Rubber Products targets a global reclaimed-rubber market growing at ~11% CAGR.

**European energy costs remain a structural margin drag, while US tariff exposure is being actively managed** — Rain Industries' CFO reports gas at "Euro 30 to 40 MMBtu… much higher than the normal levels prior to geo-political conflicts," and Advanced Materials remains weak on "European cost pressures." On the US side, Pidilite's pigments division faced "a sharp downturn… due to high reliance on the American market and tariff discussions" but believes the worst is behind with "Plan Bs built." Garware Hi-Tech Films navigated 50% US tariff increases while still posting 15% sequential growth.

**Battery-materials and energy-transition demand is reshaping input cost structures** — Rain Industries flags the battery industry as "a price-insensitive buyer of low-sulphur/low-metals GPC, permanently shifting demand dynamics and raising input costs." Gujarat Fluorochemicals is positioning as "a non-Chinese global supplier in the lithium-battery value chain." Himadri targets the 9.4-million-ton cathode/anode market by 2030. SRF notes "the industry transition to next-generation environmentally friendly refrigerants is inevitable."

> "From our discussions with stakeholders, it is evident that Chinese players are finding it difficult to sustain these price levels, and we believe that this situation is not viable in the long run. When this correction will happen, remains difficult to predict at this stage."
> — Ashish Bharat Ram, Chairman and Managing Director, SRF

> "We are exceptionally well-positioned to capitalize on emerging geopolitical tailwinds, particularly those driven by regulatory changes like the recently approved US Biosecure Act. By acquiring a US-based CDMO platform, we now offer global pharma companies an essential alternative for leading their supply chains away from restricted geographies."
> — Anand Desai, Managing Director, Anupam Rasayan India

## Newsletter synthesis — Telecommunications (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across telcos, telecom infrastructure, and telecom equipment manufacturers. Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions covering Bharti Airtel, Bharti Hexacom, Vodafone Idea, Tata Communications, HFCL, Tejas Networks, Sterlite Technologies and Pace Digitek.

**Tariff architecture, not one-off hikes, is the new ARPU playbook** — with industry-wide "tariff repair" intermittent, operators are engineering ARPU growth through plan-tier re-architecture and mix shifts. Gopal Vittal (Bharti Airtel) laid out a small/medium/large/extra-large pack structure where heavy data allowances compress and premium tiers index from 100 to ~500 (vs. 250 in India today, citing Indonesia). Shashwat Sharma (Airtel India) confirmed "in the absence of tariff repair" they are sweating ARPU via feature-phone-to-smartphone and prepaid-to-postpaid upgrades. Vodafone Idea linked ARPU growth to population-coverage expansion and smartphone conversion (33% of Vi's base is still on feature phones — "a big lever for us to push").

**5G networks are built but under-monetised — "pretty empty" and non-standalone** — despite rapid 5G coverage rollouts (Vi reached 29 cities across all 17 circles; Airtel and Jio blanketed the country), operators concede the networks are carrying little traffic and standalone (SA) deployment is being deliberately deferred. Vittal admitted Airtel's "networks on 5G are pretty empty and so we have a lot of headroom," with FWA — not mobile — earmarked as the first port of call for SA. 5G is currently a coverage-and-retention tool, not a revenue engine; SA monetisation remains a 2026+ event.

**Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) has emerged as the primary home-broadband growth engine** — FWA is repeatedly framed as the structural answer to India's low fixed-broadband penetration. Bharti Hexacom's Soumen Ray confirmed the "lion's share of homes acquisition in Q4 has been with FWA" because "there is no wired broadband in large geographies" of Rajasthan and the North-East. Airtel's group CFO ranked fibre as the "gold standard," FWA next, and UBR last. FWA is how the sector extends broadband where FTTH economics don't work.

**AGR debt and government equity are reshaping the competitive structure** — the AGR overhang has materially eased for Vodafone Idea after reassessment cut dues from ₹87,695 cr (frozen) to ₹64,046 cr (finalised), with the FY35 repayment schedule unchanged. The government has capped its equity stake at 49% (to avoid Vi becoming a PSU) while affirming the need for "three private players." Vi's leadership now declares "the worst is behind us," backed by a ₹45,000 cr three-year capex plan and an SBI-led ₹35,000 cr facility. This reframes the sector from "duopoly inevitable" to "structurally supported trio."

**The B2B / enterprise pivot is the second growth curve for every telco** — Airtel's Vittal described actively walking away from commoditised low-margin businesses while the digital portfolio grew 25–30%. Tata Communications' Ganesh Lakshminarayanan reported digital platforms now exceed 50% of data revenue and framed the opportunity around AI agents and "unified infrastructure." Vi highlighted a 10-year, 5-million-smart-meter IoT contract with GE Power as a "landmark." Enterprise digitisation and IoT are where telcos are seeking non-ARPU, higher-margin growth.

**AI-driven data traffic is the structural demand thesis underpinning the entire telecom-equipment and fibre value chain** — equipment makers HFCL, Tejas Networks, and Sterlite Technologies are all anchoring capex and capacity expansion on the same forecast: AI workloads will force a multi-year fibre/network build cycle. HFCL's Mahendra Nahata called AI-driven data flow "mammoth… leaving no other option than using fibre optic cable," with a ₹15,000 cr order book and ₹950 cr capex into preform backward integration. Tejas Networks cited AI traffic projected to exceed 60% of network traffic by 2030, driving a "build super cycle" in 400G/800G edge. Tata Communications quantified data-centre-to-data-centre connectivity in India as "at least a billion-dollar opportunity by 2030."

**Regulatory overreach on telecom vs. an under-regulated broader digital ecosystem is a recurring industry grievance** — Gopal Vittal (Bharti Airtel): "Most regulators globally… are focused mainly on the telecom sector… The overreach on telecom is far greater, while the rest remains a bit of a wild west." He argued regulation "needs to keep pace with changes in technology, and right now, it isn't." This frames the sector's demand for a level playing field with OTT and digital players.

**Data centres, sovereign cloud, and platform/SaaS monetisation are the new value-unlocking vectors** — Airtel targets ~1 GW of data-centre capacity (≈25% market share) in 3–4 years, citing "sovereign cloud" opportunity from data-localisation needs and flagging a potential listing of the data-centre entity. Tata Communications is pursuing an IPO of STT GDC's Indian asset. Airtel is also licensing its internal digital platforms to other telcos (Singtel, Globe) as a high-margin, low-capex SaaS business. Telcos are unbundling infrastructure and software assets that command higher multiples than the core mobility business.

> "Restructuring the tariff architecture is essential to improve financial health of the industry and sustain future investments. This could simply mean reducing data allowances on some of the packs and charging more for those who can afford to pay… if you compare that with a market like Indonesia… then that goes from 100 to 500."
> — Gopal Vittal, Vice Chairman & Managing Director, Bharti Airtel

> "Our three-year targets are unambiguous: sustained net customer addition, double-digit revenue growth, and tripling the EBITDA. We are backing these targets with 45,000 crores of investment, strong promoter commitment, and a leadership team that has emerged from the most challenging conditions. The worst is behind us."
> — Akshat Kishore, Chief Executive Officer, Vodafone Idea

## Sector Overview

Automotive sector encompasses vehicle manufacturing, assembly, components, and related services. This sector includes original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) for passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, two-wheelers, and electric vehicles, as well as component suppliers (ancillary manufacturers, tyre and battery makers, forgings, gears, and other Tier 1/Tier 2 parts).

## Sector Structure

The sector consolidates both vehicle OEMs and the ancillary/component supply chain (previously tracked separately as Auto_Ancillary, now merged).

## Business Description

### Vehicle Manufacturing
- **Passenger Vehicles**: Cars, SUVs, and utility vehicles
- **Commercial Vehicles**: Trucks, buses, and commercial transport vehicles
- **Two-Wheelers**: Motorcycles and scooters
- **Electric Vehicles**: EV manufacturing and development

## Newsletter synthesis — Automotive (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across OEMs (2W, 3W, PV, CV) and auto-ancillary suppliers. Sources span The Chatter, Points & Figures and Plotlines editions covering Bajaj Auto, Ashok Leyland, Bosch, Tata Motors, Mahindra & Mahindra, Hyundai Motor India, Hero MotoCorp, TVS Motor, Ather Energy, Uno Minda, Gabriel India, CEAT, Precision Camshafts, Endurance Technologies, Tenneco Clean Air India, Bharat Forge, Samvardhana Motherson, Exide Industries and others.

**EV transition is bifurcating the sector — profitable in 2W/3W, cautious in PV/CV, and global OEMs are pulling back** — Bajaj Auto reports its EV business is now "profitable" at ~20–30% of domestic revenue and is doubling Chetak capacity; TVS sees 3W EV penetration at 30–33%; M&M's BEV mix is 8.7% with new launches adding, not cannibalising, volume. Yet Precision Camshafts notes "some of the largest American OEMs have taken a complete U-turn on their EV strategy," Tenneco observes US/EU markets "sliding back from an EV to more of a compromise… hybrid or range extender," and Samvardhana Motherson's order-book EV share actually fell from 24% to 22%. India's 2W/3W electrification is running ahead of the global 4W curve, and suppliers are hedging across powertrains.

**GST rate cut (28%→18%) is the single biggest demand catalyst cited across the sector** — unlocking a delayed CV replacement cycle (Ashok Leyland: MHCV +24% in Q3, "a much-needed trigger for a fresh commercial vehicle replacement cycle," fleet age stretched from 7.5 to 10.5 years) and pulling forward 2W demand. M&M frames it as a structural reset after LCV/tractor customers absorbed "unprecedented 25–30% price increases" over five years; Suprajit calls it "a godsend." Risk flagged by Bajaj: subsequent OEM price hikes risk "rolling back 30–40%" of the GST benefit.

**Commodity inflation is sharp and broad-based, but pass-through mechanisms are largely intact, just lagged** — Bajaj's CFO quantifies steel +15%, copper +20%, aluminium/noble metals +35–45%; Endurance reports aluminium +20–25% with 100% customer compensation; CEAT flags ~10%+ RM inflation into May. Bharat Forge and Tenneco call raw material a "direct pass-through." Rare-earth magnets are a distinct chokepoint: TVS cites "magnet availability" hitting EV supply; Hero MotoCorp calls it "a challenge… covered for Q2 FY26"; Uno Minda highlights having "developed alternatives to rare earth magnets."

**Premiumisation is the structural revenue lever — content-per-vehicle is rising regardless of volume growth** — Bajaj sees ≤100cc share falling from ~55% to ~48% with 150cc+ growing at twice the industry rate; TVS notes "premium and super-premium segments are growing faster… entry-level is not." Gabriel India is shifting to inverted front forks and mono-shocks with better realisations; Uno Minda cites the LED-lighting transition lifting kit value per vehicle; Tenneco argues "premiumization of SUVs never happened… but that's changing dramatically"; Bosch sees ESP adoption plus rising sensor counts adding revenue per car.

**Export markets are simultaneously opening (China+1, EU FTA, US tariff cuts) and disrupting (Middle East, China demand collapse)** — Uno Minda and M&M flag the India-EU FTA reverting auto-component tariffs to 0% and US reciprocal tariffs falling from 50%→18%; Tenneco is explicit that "exports is going to be a very key vector… many OEMs from a China plus one diversification standpoint are looking to India." But Tata Motors lost ~20% of its Middle East international business during the Strait disruption, JLR calls China's weakness "structural and permanent," and Amara Raja could not supply the US this quarter. Bajaj and TVS are riding strong LATAM/Africa export recovery.

**Regulation is a durable, under-appreciated demand driver — CAFE 3/BS7, ADAS, Bharat NCAP, AVAS** — Tenneco sizes CAFE 3 + BS7 at "1,300–1,400 crores of additional content per vehicle" and has secured entry into the largest PV OEM's panel via gasoline particulate filters; Bosch ties growth to mandated airbags, ABS, ESP and Bharat NCAP; Uno Minda is positioned for the mandatory AVAS (acoustic alert) rule for EVs; Ashok Leyland expects ADAS to be accepted by logistics buyers.

**Localisation is now a margin strategy, a supply-chain-resilience strategy, and an export-competitiveness strategy simultaneously** — Hyundai reports localization at 84% (up from 82%) and "continuously working on localizing high-technology parts"; Tenneco holds 89–90% localization ("that's where our profit margins come from"); Bosch credits localization for its lower cost base; IPEC has 55%+ DVA but flags semiconductors/microcontrollers as a 3–5 year bottleneck; Ather and Exide are betting on Indian cell manufacturing despite limited near-term cost benefit, expecting policy to "make it worth."

**Rural demand is a bright spot and is broadening the buyer base across PVs, CVs and 2W alike** — Hyundai reports its highest-ever quarterly rural contribution to domestic sales at >24%; M&M calls growth "broad-based across rural and urban"; CEAT links robust Rabi sowing and Kharif harvest to stronger rural disposable cash flows; Ashok Leyland and Bajaj both connect inter-town commuting and road-network expansion to structural 3W/CV demand.

## Sector Overview

The Banking sector in India encompasses commercial banks, cooperative banks, and financial institutions that provide banking services to individuals and businesses. This sector forms the backbone of India's financial system.

## Sector Structure

### Parent Sector: Banking (Comprehensive Financial Services)
- **Banking** - Commercial banks, cooperative banks, foreign banks
- **Financial_Services** - NBFCs, housing finance, specialized finance (sub-sector)
- **Insurance** - Life, general, and health insurance (sub-sector)

- **Growth Drivers**: Economic growth, digital banking adoption, and financial inclusion

## Investment Considerations

### Growth Opportunities
- **Digital Banking**: Mobile banking, UPI, and digital payments growth
- **Financial Inclusion**: Expansion into rural and semi-urban areas
- **Cross-selling**: Insurance, mutual funds, and wealth management
- **MSME Lending**: Growing micro, small, and medium enterprise segment

### Risk Factors
- **Asset Quality**: Non-performing assets (NPAs) and loan defaults
- **Regulatory Changes**: RBI policy changes and compliance requirements
- **Competition**: Fintech disruption and new banking licenses
- **Interest Rate Risk**: Impact of rate changes on net interest margins
- **Economic Cycles**: Sensitivity to economic growth and credit cycles

### Market Trends
- **Digital Transformation**: Shift to online and mobile banking
- **Consolidation**: Mergers and acquisitions in banking sector
- **Green Banking**: ESG lending and sustainable finance
- **Neo-banking**: Digital-only banking platforms

## Performance Metrics

### Key Financial Ratios
- **Net Interest Margin (NIM)**: Typically 3-5% for Indian banks
- **CASA Ratio**: Current and savings account ratio (30-40% good)
- **Gross NPA**: Non-performing assets ratio (below 2% desirable)
- **Return on Assets (ROA)**: 0.5-1.5% for well-performing banks

### Industry-Specific KPIs
- **Credit Growth**: Year-on-year loan book expansion
- **Deposit Growth**: Customer deposit base expansion
- **Digital Transaction Volume**: UPI and mobile banking metrics
- **Branch Network**: Physical and digital touchpoint expansion

## Newsletter synthesis — Banking (multi-edition)

A bank-only cross-edition synthesis (distinct from the broader Financial_Services note) drawn from concall commentary across HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, State Bank of India, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Bank of Baroda, Canara Bank, Punjab National Bank, Indian Bank, Yes Bank, Bandhan Bank, AU Small Finance Bank, Federal Bank and Union Bank of India. Themes are bank-specific deltas vs. the Financial_Services synthesis: NIM guidance ranges, FY26 credit-growth guidance numbers, ECL transition capital needs, slippage behaviour by segment, PCR trajectory, CASA/LDR discipline and branch productivity.

**FY26 credit-growth guidance is being revised upward across the board, with SBI, BoB and Yes Bank explicitly hiking ranges** — SBI raised its guide from 12–14% to 13–15% for the quarter; Bank of Baroda's Debadatta Chand said "we are optimistic having a growth higher than 13%" (vs. 11–13% prior); Yes Bank's Vinay Tonse anchored to industry at "14–15%"; HDFC Bank printed 12% credit growth vs. a 10.5–11.5% system estimate. Indian Bank held at 11–13% advances growth, while AU SFB's loan book ex-unsecured grew 22% — pointing to a broad-based re-acceleration after the FY25 slowdown to ~10% system growth.

**NIM compression from deposit repricing is the universal overhang, but banks are guiding to a trough and defending it via mix** — Canara Bank guided NIM to a 2.52–2.60% band (after a 22 bps fall to 2.51%); ICICI Bank expects margins "stable from here" post the rate-cycle absorption; Union Bank of India is shedding low-yield advances to hold NIM in a 2.8–3.0% range; PNB's Ashok Chandra flagged 70% deposit repricing done by Dec'25 with the tail running into Q1 FY27; HDFC Bank called out that deposit transmission is "only about 50-60 basis points so far" vs. asset-side moves. Bandhan and AU SFB are the rare expanders, citing lower cost of funds.

**ECL (Ind AS) transition is being front-loaded — banks are pre-provisioning rather than waiting for the 5-year glide path** — PNB made ₹955 cr additional floating provisions in Q3 (total ₹1,775 cr) against an estimated ₹9,000–10,000 cr total ECL impact over 5 years (~10–15 bps/quarter recurring); Indian Bank's Binod Kumar pegged ECL impact at ~₹3,000 cr with ₹1,000 cr already provided and another ₹1,500–2,000 cr to be created this year; Bank of Baroda sees max 0.6–0.7% CRAR impact spread over 5 years (~18 bps recurring); Canara Bank estimates ~₹10,000 cr total (~₹2,000–2,500 cr p.a.). The discipline is consistent: nobody is waiting for April 2027.

**Slippages are concentrated in MSME and agriculture, with banks attributing the spike to one-off classification automation rather than systemic stress** — Canara Bank's slippages rose QoQ on MSME (₹1,250 cr vs typical ₹1,000 cr), Agri (₹750 cr) and Retail (₹650 cr); Union Bank of India explicitly blamed "full automation of our asset classification system" with "new logic parameters" driving higher MSME/agri slippages "specific to this quarter only"; Indian Bank's Binod Kumar contained MSME stress to "one or two specific accounts, not systemic." By contrast, Kotak Mahindra Bank reported fresh slippages down ~₹100 cr QoQ (excluding upgrades), residuals from CV and rural.

**Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR) is being held at or above 80% as a deliberate buffer — a clear industry posture** — Yes Bank's Niranjan Banodkar flagged "PCR remaining above 80% for the last 3 quarters" and built an additional voluntary ₹341 cr buffer rather than flowing recoveries to PAT; Indian Bank guided credit cost to remain "sub 1%" (currently 0.23%) with slippage ratio "sub 1 at 0.77"; PNB is pre-provisioning ahead of ECL. Federal Bank sits at ~75% PCR with standard restructured + net NPAs down from ₹4,035 cr (Q4 FY23) to ₹2,208 cr (Q3 FY26).

**Loan-mix rebalancing is active and deliberate: banks are exiting low-yield corporate advances and pivoting to RAM (retail-agri-MSME), gold loans and secured retail** — PNB is "selling low-yielding advances and replacing with high-yielding" ones; Indian Bank settled ~₹6,000 cr of low-yield loans in Q1 alone "mindful of the balance between growth and the bottom line"; Canara Bank grew RAM at 18.70% (8.88% yield) with gold loans +~30%; Axis Bank's Puneet Sharma set an optimal mix of "58–60% retail, 23–25% wholesale, and the balance being SME"; Yes Bank is "intentionally avoiding hyper-competitive, thin-margin loan markets (notably Home Loans and Car Loans)." Gold-loan reclassification by RBI is the swing factor for PSLC income (Canara, BoB).

**CASA recovery is the swing variable for FY26 — PSBs are reporting multi-quarter highs, but private banks flag capital-market flow as a structural drag** — Bank of Baroda's CASA growth of 8.6% was "one of the best in last 8 quarters" (savings +7.4%, current ~19%); Indian Bank's CASA grew 15.43% in Q1 FY27; HDFC Bank lifted granular <₹3 cr deposits to 47% of net accretion (from 31% in FY25) with merger-group CASA up from ~₹50,000 cr to ₹86,000 cr in 2.5 years. But Kotak's Ashok Vaswani warned that "flows in commodities and capital markets are putting more pressure on low-cost deposits," and Axis's Amitabh Chaudhry hopes deposit growth converges with credit growth "in the next 15–18 months."

**MFI cycle has troughed — collection efficiencies are normalising, but growth resumption is cautious and CGFMU-guaranteed** — AU SFB's non-overdue MFI collection efficiency hit 99.3% (99.5% for December), the highest in six quarters, with 83% of the MFI book now under the CGFMU Guarantee Scheme; management sees "green shoots" with a growth decision by April but is holding credit cards for "another one year for readjustment." Bandhan Bank is guiding credit cost down from 2% to 1.6–1.7% with ROA targeted at 1.5–1.6% by FY27 exit. The MFIN guardrails (borrowers with >3 lenders down to 4.9% GLP from 25.3%) are credited with strengthening the ecosystem.

**Capital-raise appetite is muted — internal accruals are covering both growth and ECL, and branch productivity is the new ROE lever** — Indian Bank's Binod Kumar: "I don't see any need for growth capital" despite an unused ₹5,000 cr QIP approval; Canara Bank's ~₹17,000–20,000 cr annual profit means "no QIP needed in the immediate future." Indian Bank flagged a branch productivity inflection: 51% of branches hit Q1 target vs. a historical 25–30%. HDFC Bank nearly doubled its branch network to ~9,700 with tech spend "more than quadrupled to around billion dollars," while AU SFB received in-principle RBI universal bank approval with an 18-month transition.

> "ROA is what we should focus on. ROE is an intermediate metric. If you take higher risk and bring that into the top line, you may lose it in the credit loss below the ROA line. So focus on the returns on assets."
> — Sashi Jagdishan, CEO, HDFC Bank

> "We have made additional floating provisions of INR 955 crores on a prudential basis in Q3 on account of which credit cost is looking elevated at 0.46%. Now total floating provision is INR 1,775 crores, which will help us in moving towards ECL transition."
> — Ashok Chandra, MD and CEO, Punjab National Bank

## The Chatter — RBI's Governor, Canara Bank, IRCTC, & More

*Regulator keynote — FIBAC 2026 address by RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra on the AI playbook for Indian banking.*

**AI is the defining theme of the decade — on par with liberalisation and digitalisation:** Governor Malhotra frames AI as the structural shift that "will define this decade of Indian banking" the way liberalisation defined the 1990s and digitalisation the 2010s — a change in how banks evaluate risk, serve customers and price capital, not a technology to be procured.

**AI changes the economics of credit delivery:** Alternative data (GST filings, utility payments, digital footprints) extends the "frontier of bankable India" to new-to-credit borrowers, gig workers and small enterprises at a fraction of the marginal cost of manual underwriting — opening retail and MSME growth segments.

**Operational efficiency is the clearest near-term lever:** Document processing, reconciliation, internal audit sampling and regulatory return preparation are "ripe for AI-assisted automation" — scope to reduce cost-to-income ratios / intermediation costs across operations, sales, customer service and credit & collections.

**Fraud defence flips from rules to real-time ML:** Fraud now moves at the speed of an API call; rules-based engines are perpetually one step behind, so machine-learning models that continuously learn from transaction patterns and flag anomalies in real time become the counter.

**Three risks the regulator is watching:** (1) black-box opacity — inability to explain credit decisions undermines accountability; (2) concentration/herding — a shared foundation-model/vendor layer turns one bank's error into a systemic one and can amplify stressed-market volatility; (3) erosion of human judgment — "the model decided" is never an acceptable answer; human oversight to explain, intervene and override must be a design principle.

**What banks must do now:** Board-approved AI governance policies with accountability for outcomes, explainability for materially customer-affecting decisions, red-teaming and stress-testing AI before and during deployment. Regulatory roadmap: standardised credit-risk approach, ECL framework, EIR changes, project finance / related-party / dividend / NOP norms, and all applicable Basel III guidelines live from April 1, 2027 on a calibrated glide path.

> "The theme of the conference – Artificial Intelligence - has been well chosen. It is apt and timely. It is a theme that, I believe, will define this decade of Indian banking as decisively liberalisation defined the 1990s and digitalisation defined the 2010s."
> — Shri Sanjay Malhotra, Governor, Reserve Bank of India

## Newsletter synthesis — Capital_Markets (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across AMCs (HDFC, ICICI Pru, Nippon Life), exchanges (BSE), depositories/registrars (CDSL, NSDL, KFin, CAMS), wealth managers & brokers (Anand Rathi Wealth, Anand Rathi S&S, Geojit, Fivepaisa, Nuvama, Emkay), and rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA). Sources span The Chatter, Plotlines and Points & Figures editions.

**Industry AUM is compounding near 25% YoY on the back of structural financialization, but management frames India as still "very early stage":** Anand Rathi Share & Stock Brokers flagged industry MF AUM climbing from ₹65.7L cr (Mar 2025) to ~₹82L cr (Feb 26), +24.8% YoY; KFin's investor-presentation data shows India MF AUM at ₹81T (+18.1% YoY) with equity AUM at ₹46.7T (+17.8%). HDFC AMC's Navneet Munot called India "at a very early stage of financialization of savings," while Anand Rathi S&S explicitly tagged the trend a "structural tailwind." Demat accounts rose from 15.14 cr (Mar 24) to 22.2 cr (Feb 26) per Anand Rathi S&S, corroborated by CDSL crossing the 20 cr industry milestone.

**Equity-AUM mix is tilting passive, and SIPs are emerging as the annuity-like revenue base:** ICICI Pru AMC reported passive AUM growth of ~40% YoY and "systematic transaction volumes more than doubled over two years," explicitly framing it as a shift to "stable, annuity-like revenue streams (SIP/STP)." Nippon Life AMC's Gold & Silver ETF combined AUM hit ~₹688bn (+54% QoQ), crossing ₹1T in Jan 2026, and the company launched the GIFT City–linked Nifty 50 BeES for Japanese NISA investors. KFin reported SIP monthly inflows at ₹310 bn (Dec 25) with 101M SIP folios. ICICI Pru AMC noted "digital natives maintaining and even stepping up SIPs through market dips."

**TER regulation is a measurable but absorbable headwind — and a consolidation tailwind for scaled players:** ICICI Pru AMC quantified the new TER regime (effective Apr 1) at "~3–4 bps gross" compression, to be "offset via distributor renegotiation and cost optimization." HDFC AMC went further: it called the sliding-scale TER "inevitable TER compression," tagged the removal of the additional 5 bps exit-load TER as "materially negative industry-wide," but argued lower TERs "improve alpha and favour larger funds — a consolidation tailwind for established players." Both AMCs are leaning on operating leverage rather than pricing power.

**Alternatives (AIF/PMS/SIF) are the new high-margin battleground across AMCs and wealth managers:** HDFC AMC completed the first close of a structured credit fund (~₹13 bn) and is expanding into PMS and alternatives; Nippon Life AMC's AIF raised cumulative commitments of ₹81 bn (Q1 FY26 was its highest quarterly raise ever at ₹7 bn) across performing credit, real estate and a VC FoF with 395+ startup exposures, and is launching SIF as a "separate business vertical" under Andrew Holland. ICICI Pru AMC is acquiring fund-management rights from ICICI Venture to expand into PE/VC, with Assets under Advisory at ₹320.86 bn (Dec 25); Anand Rathi Wealth runs a "distribution-first, manufacturing-later" model with 99% of structured products carrying rollover options.

**Exchange volumes are being driven by the derivatives cycle, not cash markets — and BSE is capturing disproportionate share:** BSE's average daily premium turnover in index derivatives hit a record ₹19,523 cr in FY26 (~+118% YoY from ~₹8,9xx cr), co-location revenue doubled (₹74 cr → ₹171 cr), and the FPI count grew from 100 to 520 with a ~800 target. BSE's Sundararaman Ramamurthy positioned the franchise as "evolving… into the country's definitive super gateway for long-term wealth" via STAR NPS. Fivepaisa's management confirms the broker-side mix: "80% is for derivatives and 20% is in the cash segment… in line with most of the other discount brokers." ICICI Pru AMC noted BSE/NSE cash-market growth remains "constrained by pending Smart Order Routing approvals" — i.e., derivatives are carrying the volume cycle.

**Brokers are deliberately rebalancing toward non-broking income to escape the derivative-volume cycle:** Anand Rathi Share & Stock Brokers is "intentionally diversifying income toward a 50:50 split between non-broking and broking segments," with distribution income +44.1% YoY to ₹1,129M and a fresh corporate-agency license for insurance. Fivepaisa's CFO argued float income is structurally embedded in broking economics: "if hypothetically tomorrow… [SEBI] says that you can't enjoy float income, it would be difficult for every discount broker to go for a trade of INR 20 per order." Geojit is "deliberately conservative on derivatives" and pivoting to a private-wealth target of ₹20,000 cr AUM (from ₹2,500 cr).

**Wealth-management AUM is crossing psychological milestones and is being scaled linearly via RM hiring, not via product margins:** Anand Rathi Wealth crossed the ₹1 lakh cr AUM mark and posted its 18th consecutive quarter of >20% YoY PAT growth, framing the model as "linear, not exponential… tied to RM hiring and training," with FY27 guidance of ₹1,415 cr revenue / ₹460 cr PAT and industry-best ROE of 46.74%. Geojit is scaling RMs from 54 to ~100 to drive its 8x private-wealth AUM target. Nuvama Wealth sized the opportunity: India's professionally-managed wealth is 15% (vs 75% in the US), "expected to triple in the next five years." Emkay Global has obtained a Dubai branch license (Dec 24) to extend its PMS/AIF franchise internationally — wealth is going cross-border.

**Depositories and registrars are riding the same demat-account wave but with diverging revenue mix:** CDSL added 6.5M accounts in Q2 FY26 alone at an 80% demat-market share, with diversification into insurance repository (Centrico) and direct-to-customer portals; NSDL holds 73% of the unlisted-issuer segment, onboards 11,500+ companies, and runs NSDL Payments Bank (3M+ customers, ₹400 cr deposits). KFin positions as a "multi-asset servicing platform" with 1,717 registered AIFs and 17M pension subscribers; CAMS operates the digiLoan, CAMSfinserv and CAMSPay rails. The revenue mix is bifurcating: NSDL disclosed 43% recurring / 57% non-recurring, while CDSL leans on transaction + account-maintenance charges — both are exposed to market-cycle participation but with different elasticity.

So, we would much rather be thoughtful and deliberate."

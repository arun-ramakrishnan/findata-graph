## The Chatter — TCS, Indian Bank, Amagi, CMR Green & More

**India's microfinance cycle blows up every 4–5 years, almost always industry-wide** — investors have priced the boom-bust rhythm in from the start; cyclicality is a feature the market underwrites, not a bug it tries to fix. Returns demanded during the good years therefore stay elevated.

**The latest crisis was self-inflicted, not external** — unlike prior cycles (politics, calamity, single rogue lender), this one was engineered by the industry itself after RBI freed MFI interest rates in 2022. Fourfold loan-book expansion (FY17–FY24) was followed by active borrower accounts falling 31% (87 mn → 69 mn).

**Secured-loan limit raised 15% → 25% → 40%** — less a reform than RBI's "candid admission" of how often the sector falls into trouble; effectively a balance-sheet rescue mechanism.

**Credit culture is the structural risk** — borrower base is exposed to natural calamities, political interference and loan-waiver promises that break repayment discipline even among those who can pay. "A whole village refusing to pay because they think they won't have to is a broken culture."

**The only leading signal that matters is underwriting quality** — visible long before it surfaces in the NPA line. The Raghuram Rajan AQR ("forced striptease") laid bare 30%+ bad loans at two banks; today system net NPA is <1% because underwriting was overhauled.

**Path forward is unsentimental consolidation** — many mid-sized MFIs are quietly becoming business correspondents (BCs), the smallest are shutting down, and even the largest need to "reinvent themselves."

> "You give the borrowers more money, push down their throat, which they are not able to pay back. It was a completely supply-side problem. You can't blame the borrowers… It was sort of harakiri — because they thought that good times will last."
> — Tamal Bandyopadhyay, Senior Advisor, Jana Small Finance Bank

*Source: The Chatter — TCS, Indian Bank, Amagi, CMR Green & More*

## Newsletter synthesis — NBFC (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across diversified NBFCs (Bajaj Finance, Shriram, L&T Finance, Piramal, Tata Capital, Cholamandalam), gold-loan specialists (Muthoot Finance, Manappuram, IIFL), microfinance (CreditAccess Grameen, Muthoot Microfin), HDB Financials, Sammaan Capital, and Jio Financial. Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions.

**AUM growth guidance stays aggressive at the top of the LBFC pack even as the cycle resets:** Bajaj Finance's "22–24% balance-sheet growth," Shriram Finance's 18–20% post-MUFG target (up from 16–17%), Muthoot Finance's 50%+ YoY gold-loan AUM print (₹1,39,658 cr standalone, ₹1,65,000 cr consolidated), L&T Finance's "20–22%" FY26 target, Piramal's ₹1.5L cr by FY28 (~24–26% CAGR), HDB's "nominal plus 6–7%" framework, and CreditAccess Grameen's "something over 20%" — i.e. demand-pull, share-gain stories dominate guidance, with Cholamandalam explicitly framing downturn as "the best time to basically gain market share" and Mahindra Finance (per Dr. Anish Shah) pencilled to grow AUM 4x between FY20–FY31. The lone caution: Sundaram-style excess liquidity (Shriram's ₹31,000 cr, ~20–25 bps NIM drag) signals growth could outrun deployment in the near term.

**Cost of funds is the swing variable for NIM trajectory — with rating upgrades and liability franchise reshaping being the dominant lever:** Shriram expects ~100 bps borrowing-cost reduction over two years from the MUFG deal; Piramal's AA→AA+ upgrade is expected to lower cost of borrowing "50–80 basis points"; L&T Finance leveraged India's sovereign upgrade to a BBB international rating to "diversify funding" offshore; HDB consciously ran "very low CP levels as dry powder" with bank loans "below 50%" of the mix. Muthoot, by contrast, flagged that "banks have not significantly reduced their MCLR" — so liability-side repricing is lagging the asset-side rate-cut cycle. Muthoot also held rates "by about 0.5% to 1% across certain loan types" to lift yields.

**Asset quality is troughing unevenly — the unsecured/MFI cycle is past peak stress, but legacy books continue to bleed:** Tata Capital's Rajiv Sabharwal flagged "improving trends on quality as far as slippages are concerned" and is scaling personal loans, business loans and microfinance again; HDB expects "positive momentum on our unsecured business"; Muthoot Microfin's credit cost fell from 9.4% (FY25) → 4.3% (Q1) → 3.6% with overdue collections tripling to ₹19 cr/month; CreditAccess reported Karnataka "coming back very, very strong to our normal levels"; L&T Finance's Sachin Joshi said collections are "back at 99.05% in Karnataka, and overall at 99.5%". Bajaj Finance is the cleanest example of legacy drag: its captive 2W/3W book is "<1% of AUM, but in Q4 it accounted for 13% of GNPA and 5% of overall credit cost" and will run down to <₹1,500 cr by Sep 2026. Cholamandalam explicitly called FY26 "a year of collection, not the year of disbursement".

**RBI remains the dominant external force — from gold-loan LTV liberalisation and co-lending overhaul to risk-weight and personal-loan signalling:** Muthoot's George Alexander Muthoot hailed the LTV revision (75% → 85% for tickets up to ₹2.5L, covering "~85% of customers") as "quite gold loan business friendly"; Manappuram's BP Nandakumar said RBI draft guidelines "will not disrupt gold loan industry". Sammaan Capital's Gagan Banga detailed the August 2025 co-lending rewrite — extending beyond priority sector, allowing NBFC-to-NBFC arrangements, and cutting minimum retention from 20% → 10%. Shriram's Y.S. Chakravarti was candid that the personal-loan slowdown was regulator-driven, not credit-driven: "the regulator was expressing concern on the personal loan growth in the industry".

**Gold loans are the cross-industry secular bright spot — every diversified NBFC is entering or scaling gold, partly as a hedge against unsecured stress:** Muthoot Finance leads with record AUM and a 57% LTV ("a large margin", with making charges adding 15–20% to replacement cost); IIFL's gold book "fully bounced back from last year's embargo, reaching an all time high in AUM"; Bajaj Finance grew gold AUM 115% and expects it to "cross 10% of total AUM by FY27"; L&T Finance acquired Paul Merchants' gold business (130 branches) citing a captive cross-sell base of ₹17,000 cr of microfinance customers' gold loans; Manappuram is shifting mix towards gold with MFI targeted down to 10%; Muthoot Microfin leverages Muthoot Pappachan group infra for gold "without additional investment". Driver: Nirmal Jain (IIFL) summed it up — "MSME enterprises… now that they have no source of funding, so they might be taking gold loans".

**Microfinance cycle is being called at the trough — but recovery is graded, not V-shaped:** Multiple players — CreditAccess, Muthoot Microfin, L&T Finance, Tata Capital, HDB — point to green shoots in collection efficiency, MFIN guardrails biting (CreditAccess: GLP of >3-lender borrowers collapsed from 25.3% → 4.9%; approval rates down from 65% → 55–60% for new borrowers), and credit costs rolling over. L&T Finance's Sudipta Roy: "the microfinance industry was going through an asset quality issue… that is a passing phase, the industry has more or less resumed its journey on coming back to normalcy". Muthoot Microfin and CreditAccess both frame the next phase as "meaningful financial inclusion" / "journey of entrepreneurship" — i.e. higher ticket sizes, fewer over-leveraged borrowers.

**Digital/AI underwriting is moving from buzzword to operating-leverage engine — and is being quantified at the P&L level:** L&T Finance's "Project Cyclops" (1 mn lines of code, 55+ algorithms, ~⅔ of two-wheeler book underwritten digitally) delivered GNS of 7.15% — "the lowest ever" — during peak festive volumes, with Cyclops-underwritten SME loans running 160 bps lower GNS than the control book. Bajaj Finance processed 600,000 loans on a single Diwali day ("without AI our capacity was 100,000"), has 27 AI voice/text bots live, and is moving all customer engagement to bot interfaces by June 2026 with AI call-centre agents at roughly one-third human cost. HDB has >50% of early-stage collection nudges handled by bots (improving collection efficiency by ~25 bps in early buckets). Tata Capital's underwriting-assist platform cut credit-memo time from 2 days to 20 minutes (85% adoption, +30% productivity). Mahindra Group expects ₹4,100 cr FY27 revenue impact from AI, including "₹10,000 crores more in disbursements" at Mahindra Finance.

**Funding-mix diversification and capital recycling are reshaping balance sheets — away from short-term CP and towards sticky, rated liabilities:** CreditAccess flagged <60% bank-term-loan dependency as a structural advantage during the credit cycle; HDB cut CP and pushed bank borrowings below 50% of the mix; Piramal is recycling wholesale repayments (~50% of FY27 contractual already received) into higher-yield retail; L&T Finance raised international ratings to access global capital markets; Muthoot is funding branch expansion from internal cash rather than expensive new debt; Sammaan Capital positioned to benefit from expanded co-lending (lower capital consumption). The shift is deliberate and shared: from "AUM-at-any-cost" to risk-calibrated, liability-franchise-led growth.

> "Over the last 18 months, we have calibrated those responses. From here, you should see us in a cruise mode. At 5,10,000 crores, we are a significant player with a fiduciary responsibility to remain resilient. We intend to deliver a 20-22% ROE and a 22-24% balance sheet growth."
> — Rajeev Jain, Vice Chairman and Managing Director, Bajaj Finance

> "The slowing down is not because we are worried about the quality. It is also because the regulator was expressing concern on the personal loan growth in the industry… the reason why we are back on the pedal now is we have not seen any reason to be concerned in the last two quarters."
> — Y.S. Chakravarti, MD & CEO, Shriram Finance

> "Our 57% LTV provides a large margin. Additionally, ornaments have making charges that add 15-20% to the replacement cost for the customer. We have not seen people abandon gold due to price fluctuations in the last 15 years."
> — George Alexander Muthoot, Managing Director, Muthoot Finance

> "When the market is looking bad, manufacturers are also down. And at that point in time, if they are coming to us, that is the best time to basically gain market share."
> — Ravindra Kundu, MD & CEO, Cholamandalam Investment and Finance

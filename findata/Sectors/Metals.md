## Newsletter synthesis — Metals (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across steel, aluminium, zinc and other base-metals producers. Sources span The Chatter, Points & Figures and Plotlines editions covering Tata Steel, JSW Steel, Hindustan Zinc, Hindalco, Vedanta, Jindal Steel, Jindal Stainless, NMDC, MOIL and National Aluminium.

**Steel pricing cycle has bottomed; safeguard duty and recovering demand support a Q4 FY26 pickup** — domestic flat-product prices are widely described as having hit a 5-year cyclic low in Dec 2025 (Tata Steel), with prices recovering from mid-December (Jindal Steel) and India flipping to a net steel exporter in Oct '25. The Dec 2025 three-year safeguard duty (12%→11% taper, ~11.5% effective) is seen as modest versus the 25–50% protection applied globally, limiting downside and underpinning domestic realisations.

**Chinese export surge is the dominant global overhang** — China's record 119 mt of steel exports in CY25 is flagged repeatedly as the cap on global prices (Tata Steel, Jindal Steel, JSW Steel) even as Chinese domestic prices stayed rangebound on production cuts. India remained a net importer through Q2 FY26 with imports up 36% QoQ, before the safeguard duty and seasonal demand turned the trade balance.

**Firm coking coal and iron ore are squeezing spreads** — iron ore above $100/t and coking coal above $200/t kept steel spreads tight globally. Producers are responding with backward integration — Jindal Steel commissioned an additional coke-oven battery in Nov 2025 and sources 15–20% of coking coal from owned African mines; Tata Steel is pushing for a planned (not abrupt) Netherlands coke-oven transition. NMDC is branding and blending iron ore to defend realisations; MOIL notes manganese ore prices remain captive to the steel cycle.

**Massive India capacity build-out — a "nation-building" volume runway** — JSW Steel targets 51.5 mtpa by FY31 and 62 mt by FY32 (₹2.26 lakh cr capex envelope to FY33); Tata Steel sees India optionality scaling to ~60 mt (Odisha 14M→35M plus Maharashtra); NMDC is marching toward a 100 mt iron-ore goal with FY capex at an all-time high of ~₹3,300 cr. Tata Steel's TV Narendren frames the sector as investing ₹40,000–50,000 cr annually — "more than most other sectors." JSW's Jayant Acharya expects India's share of global steel consumption to rise from ~9% to ~16% in a decade as per-capita use crosses ~100 kg.

**Energy transition is reshaping aluminium/copper demand and the power-cost curve** — Hindalco cites domestic copper demand +10% (401 KT in Q3 FY26) and TCRCs falling to zero for 2026 Antofagasta contracts, signalling a structural concentrate deficit; Vedanta reports "double-digit" demand growth for both aluminium and zinc and is building a 30-lakh-ton "green aluminium" complex. Hindalco is scaling renewables to 522 MW by FY26-end; Vedanta touts pit-head power as the lowest-cost route. CBAM is not yet a barrier for Indian aluminium because power emissions aren't included (Hindalco — Satish Pai).

**CBAM definitive phase (1 Jan 2026) is a structural tailwind for EU-exposed producers** — it embeds carbon costs into EU imports, structurally favouring local producers and letting EU prices decouple from cheaper Asian levels (Tata Steel — Koushik Chatterjee). UK EAF commitment is firm despite losses, because delaying capex only prolongs red ink.

**Zinc, silver and critical-minerals diversification is the Hind Zinc / Vedanta playbook** — Hindustan Zinc posted its lowest quarterly zinc cost ($903/t) since going underground; silver now drives 44% of profits with the metal at all-time highs >$93/toz; the company has secured three critical mineral blocks (potash, tungsten, rare earths). Vedanta is scaling HZL from 1.2M to 3M tons and targeting 3,000 tons of silver (vs 800 today), explicitly framing the demerger into 5–6 listed entities to unlock sum-of-parts value.

**Downstream value-added mix shift as a cycle-dampening strategy** — producers are deliberately tilting away from commodity HRC toward value-added products to stabilise margins. Tata Steel is "selling less hot-rolled commodity steel" in favour of cold-rolled, galvanised and packaging steel, targeting ~2x overall market share in attractive segments; Jindal Steel is rebalancing toward 50% flat / 50% long products and prioritising thick HRC sections for higher EBITDA per ton; Jindal Stainless sees broad-based demand from railways (Vande Bharat, Amrit Bharat), autos and process industries, supported by the new BIS IS:17900 quality standard pushing out low-grade imports.

> "India is going through a nation-building phase with steel being a key building block for growth. This creates a long runway for steel demand to outpace the real GDP growth in the country."
> — Jayant Acharya, Joint MD and CEO, JSW Steel

> "On January 1st, 2026, CBAM entered its definitive phase with carbon costs being embedded into imports and structurally improving the competitive landscape for the EU producers."
> — Koushik Chatterjee, ED & CFO, Tata Steel

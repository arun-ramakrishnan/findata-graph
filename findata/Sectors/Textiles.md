The Textiles sector in India encompasses a wide range of activities including fiber production, textile manufacturing, apparel production, and technical textiles. This sector plays a crucial role in India's manufacturing landscape and export performance.

## Sub-Sectors

### Traditional Textiles
- Cotton textiles and garments
- Silk textiles
- Woolen textiles
- Handloom products

### Technical Textiles
- Technical fabrics and specialized textiles
- Industrial textiles
- Non-woven textiles

### Apparel Manufacturing
- Ready-made garments
- Fashion accessories
- Sportswear and activewear

### Textile Machinery
- Spinning machinery
- Weaving machinery
- Knitting machinery
- Processing and finishing equipment

## Key Characteristics

### Labor Intensive
- High employment generation potential
- Significant contribution to rural livelihoods

### Export Oriented
- Strong export performance in traditional and technical textiles
- Growing global competitiveness

### Raw Material Dependent
- Cotton, jute, silk, wool, and synthetic fibers
- Vulnerability to raw material price fluctuations

## Recent Developments

### Government Initiatives
- Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes
- Technical textiles promotion
- Handloom and handicrafts support programs

### Market Trends
- Growing demand for technical textiles
- Increasing focus on sustainability
- Digital transformation of traditional textile manufacturers

## Challenges

### Competition
- Intense competition from global manufacturers
- Price volatility in raw materials
- Need for technology upgradation

### Opportunities
- Technical textiles growth potential
- Value-added products development
- Export market expansion

## Newsletter synthesis — Textiles (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across spinning mills, fabric manufacturers, garment exporters, home textiles, technical textiles, and apparel brands. Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions covering Vardhman Textiles, Sportking India, Sanathan Textiles, Arvind Limited, Pearl Global Industries, Page Industries, Raymond Lifestyle, Dollar Industries, Varvee Global, Ganesha Ecosphere, Vedant Fashions, Cantabil Retail, Responsive Industries, Garware Technical Fibres and others.

**Indian cotton is structurally overpriced vs. global benchmarks, eroding mill competitiveness** — MSP-driven procurement by CCI has made domestic cotton 6–8 cents/lb dearer than landed costs in Vietnam/Indonesia (~80–84 cents vs. 76–78). Vardhman Textiles (Neeraj Jain) and Sportking India (Munish Awasti) both flagged this as the single biggest margin headwind for spinners. The reinstatement of the 11% import duty on cotton from January 1, 2026 worsens the disadvantage versus Bangladesh and Vietnam, which enjoy duty-free imports.

**US export demand is soft, with buyers delaying and downsizing orders amid tariff uncertainty** — Vardhman reported US customers delaying orders 30–45 days and cutting quantities 20–25%, shifting to hand-to-mouth buying. Arvind Limited absorbed ~₹23 cr of tariff-related discounts in a single quarter and is conservatively factoring in a US consumption slowdown. Pearl Global Industries noted that Walmart is ramping India sourcing but India is "just moving in" to global competitiveness.

**India is a marginal net beneficiary of the China+1 / tariff realignment, but execution is uneven** — Sanathan Textiles (Sammir Dattani) argued India is "similar or cheaper than Bangladesh" and tariffs favoring India could expand global textile market share. Sportking called India "marginal favorites and better placed compared to some of the most prominent Asian textile giants." Responsive Industries positioned itself as "India's only scaled, export-ready manufacturer" in vinyl flooring, with the US LVP market dominated by Vietnam/China (~84% of imports) and India under 1%. Garware Technical Fibres and Borana Weaves both frame themselves as PLI-scheme beneficiaries in technical textiles.

**Capacity rationalisation in spinning is deep and partly permanent — while competitors expand** — Vardhman's Neeraj Jain flagged ~11 million spindles (~1,000 units) shut down in India, with 80–90% unlikely to restart even if conditions improve: "India is not expanding at all and wherever these opportunities are there the other countries are expanding." This contrasts with Varvee Global pivoting denim capacity to non-denim at 90% utilization and Candour Techtex planning capacity expansion in blackout coating/lamination.

**Domestic demand is recovering on income-tax cuts, GST rationalization, and festive/wedding momentum — but pricing power remains weak** — Raymond Lifestyle (Amit Agarwal) called income-tax cuts and GST reduction on apparel below ₹2,500 "key catalysts for volume-led growth." Arvind's domestic B2C textile business grew >20%. Indian Terrain, Cantabil, and Dollar Industries all flagged Q3 festive/wedding-led recovery and GST tailwinds. However, Dollar Industries admitted price hikes are "very difficult, given the competitive nature of what the industry is going through" — growth must come from cost savings and premiumization.

**Technical textiles and man-made fibres (MMF) are the structural growth bet, supported by PLI and infra capex** — Sanathan Textiles is building a greenfield Punjab facility focused on polyester filament yarns, calling MMF "the future." Garware Technical Fibres highlighted geosynthetics demand from FY27 Railways capex (₹2.8 lakh cr), MoRTH (₹3.9 lakh cr), and Bharatmala (34,800 km). Candour Techtex and Borana Weaves both emphasized PLI-driven opportunity in coated/laminated fabrics and synthetic grey fabric. Ganesha Ecosphere defended margins in recycled PSF, expecting the demand-supply gap to persist 3–4 years.

**Sustainability / ESG mandates from global buyers are driving recycled-content investment, but volumes remain small** — Arvind Limited (Punit Lalbhai) noted recycled products are <2–3% of global textile volume but Europe's pending legislation on recycled content makes this a future-ready necessity. Garware, Ganesha Ecosphere, and Sanathan (Sanathan Reviro recycled brand) are all building capacity. This is a compliance-driven pull, not yet a volume-driven one.

**Premiumization and digital marketing are the twin levers for domestic apparel brands protecting margins** — Dollar Industries' Force NXT grew 26.5% in value / 48.1% in volume in Q3 FY26, with premium categories ~29% of FY25 revenue. Vedant Fashions (Vedant Modi) is pivoting to "12 to 13 mini campaigns" instead of one or two big ones. Page Industries (V.S. Ganesh) emphasized that few Indian factories can match its bonded-tech supply chain at scale.

> "The duty-free import window from August to December 31, 2025, resulted in higher cost imports because imported cotton was relatively cheaper. However, from January 1, the 11% import duty has been reinstated. This puts us at a relative disadvantage to competing countries like Bangladesh and Vietnam, which continue to enjoy duty-free imports."
> — Neeraj Jain, Joint Managing Director, Vardhman Textiles

> "If the current tariffs are relatively more favourable towards India, I think we have a damn good opportunity in front of us to really expand our global market share of textile supply. Textiles are the second largest exports out of India… we are similar or cheaper than Bangladesh."
> — Sammir Dattani, Executive Director, Sanathan Textiles

## Newsletter synthesis — Renewables (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across solar module/cell (Waaree, Emmvee, Vikram Solar, Premier Energies, Websol), wind (Suzlon, Inox Wind), solar EPC (WRTL, KPI Green, Solarium, Sterling & Wilson), and IPP/merchant (Adani Green, Acme Solar). Sources span The Chatter, Points & Figures and Plotlines editions.

**ALMM is the single most-cited sector catalyst — and it's moving upstream:** Waaree (Amit Paithankar), Premier Energies (Vinay Rustagi), Emmvee, Websol, and Vikram Solar all frame ALMM as the structural driver. The narrative is consistent: ALMM-I (modules, 173 GW listed) is settled; ALMM-II (cells, 26 GW listed, effective June 2026) is the near-term inflection that opens up corporate and IPP demand beyond DCR; ALMM-III (wafers, June 2028) is the next wall. As Rustagi (Premier) puts it, ALMM on cells means "more market segments, namely the corporate segment as well as the IPP segment, opening up." Paithankar (Waaree) calls June-2026 cell ALMM the change that "will have a positive impact overall on our business."

**Domestic cell capacity is the binding constraint, not modules:** Multiple voices agree module capacity is oversupplied (Vikram Solar deck: "~2.3x demand") while cells are underpenetrated. Emmvee's deck projects cell demand rising from ~20 GW (FY26) to >40 GW (FY27) against current capacity of ~18.5 GW — a deliberate scarcity. Vikram Solar and Waaree (PLI 6 GW ingot-wafer-cell tranche on schedule for 2027) are both racing backward into cells/wafers. Solarium's Himanshu Garg pushes back on the "overcapacity" narrative, noting utilization is "anywhere between 70% to 75%" and "module supply timelines remain challenging" — i.e., the bottleneck is real, not just a headline number.

**China de-risking is explicit and quantified, but wafer/cell upstream remains the hard part:** Emmvee's deck projects India's solar import dependence falling from 65% (FY18) to 1-5% by FY30, helped by ~40% module / 27.5% cell basic customs duty plus US tariffs on China/SEA. Premier's Saluja flags "very strong demand for non-Chinese wafers and ingot" justifying its Taiwan SAS JV. On wind, the symmetry is striking: Suzlon's Chalasani notes "India is a net exporter of wind turbine components," and Inox Wind's Sanjiv Agarwal says the new wind DCR via ALMM "bring[s] in a level playing field between the domestic manufacturers and certain Chinese players." Vikram Solar's deck distils it: "The world cannot de-risk from China without India. There is no third option at scale."

**US export window is live but tactically split: cells yes, modules careful:** Waaree's Sonal Shrivastava confirms IRA credits run through 2030 and Amit Paithankar confirms a deliberately tariff-optimised supply chain — "we are not using our own domestic manufactured cells for United States because that would attract tariffs much higher than if we source it from some other country." Premier's Saluja takes the opposite cell-only stance: "we would like to restrict ourselves to exporting cells to the US because we do not want to compete with our clients in the US." Emmvee's deck projects exports rising to 25-32% of production. The economics are explicitly tied to UFLPA / FEOC compliance — this is policy-arbitrage, not cost-arbitrage.

**Wind order books and turbine pricing power are at multi-year highs; wind is now cost-competitive vs solar+storage:** Suzlon's wind order book hit ~6.4 GW by Jan'26, with EPC scope raised to 27%. Chalasani gives the definitive tariff math: "Pure wind is 3.6 to 3.7... Solar is now let's say around 2.5 to 2.6, domestic 2.56 plus the battery cost. So unless the battery cost is less than 1 rupees 25 paisa per kilowatt hour solar plus storage never replace wind." Rahul Jain (Suzlon CFO) targets the industry "surpass[ing] 10 gigawatt over the next 2 years" en route to 100 GW by 2030, with Suzlon selling 3.15 MW and a new 3.3 MW variant while the 5 MW is still in proto.

**Storage is moving from optional to mandated — and is the new EPC frontier:** The 5%-BESS-mandatory rule for solar in Rajasthan is cited verbatim by KPI Green's Dr. Alok Das. Waaree (Amit Paithankar) is committing ~₹8,000 cr to BESS citing India's 236 GWh-by-2032 target. WRTL's management calls BESS "one of the areas where we have an opportunity to do the EPC business" and is "actively participating in all kind of possible bids." Premier (Saluja) is building 12 GWh BESS in two phases (FY27/FY28). Vikram Solar targets 15 GWh BESS by FY30; Acme Solar has commissioned a 10 MWh BESS pilot in Rajasthan. Chalasani (Suzlon) puts delivered storage energy at ~₹6.5-7.5/kWh — framing wind+storage hybrid as the cheaper RTC route.

**Solar EPC is bifurcating: commodity EPC under margin pressure, IPP/DCR-anchored EPC expanding:** WRTL (Manmohan Sharma) flags the GST 12%→5% cut as capex-relief that "will further drive the EPC demand" and is chasing a 27 GW pipeline. KPI Green is deliberately shifting its IPP/CPP revenue mix toward 25% IPP, where EBITDA margins reach "85 to 90%... CPP brings around 20 to 25." Solarium posted +43% revenue, +65% gross margin, +36% EBITDA in H1 FY26. Sterling & Wilson's order inflow climbed from ₹4,387 cr (FY23) to ₹7,051 cr (FY25), order book 84.5% India — i.e., EPC winners are scaling, but the margin delta is increasingly IPP/storage-tied rather than vanilla solar EPC.

**Green hydrogen remains in pilot/policy-stage, not yet a revenue driver for the listed renewables names:** HPCL's Visakh refinery 370 TPA green-H₂ plant (commissioned, "first Green Hydrogen plant in any Indian refinery") and the 5,000 TPA / 4,300 TPA projects at Visakh/HRRL are the most concrete examples — but HPCL is an Energy-sector refiner, not a pure-play renewable. Within renewables, only WRTL names "green hydrogen electrolyzer manufacturing" as an expansion area, without quantification. Adani Green's deck cites green hydrogen as a CAD-reduction lever at the macro level. *[Inference]*: For the 21 renewables companies, green H₂ is a 2027+ optionality, not a current thesis.

> "Pure wind is 3.6 to 3.7. In fact, even if you look at last three bids, which got broken up awarded in the first 3 months in this financial year is average is about 3.76. That's a tariff of wind. Solar is now let's say around 2.5 to 2.6, domestic 2.56 plus the battery cost. So unless the battery cost is less than 1 rupees 25 paisa per kilowatt hour solar plus storage never replace wind as simple as that."
> — JP Chalasani, Group CEO, Suzlon Energy

> "The recently notified DCR for wind through ALMM is a very strong boost for local manufacturing... bringing in a level playing field between the domestic manufacturers and certain Chinese players who were importing components today. This is a very positive move at the right time and we believe that India's supply chain is self-sufficient to cater the incremental demand coming due to this policy."
> — Sanjiv Agarwal, CEO, Inox Wind

> — Vinay Rustagi, Chief Business Officer, Premier Energies

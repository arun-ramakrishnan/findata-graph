## Newsletter synthesis — Hospitals (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across hospital chains (Apollo, Max Healthcare, Fortis, Narayana, Aster DM). HCG (Healthcare Global Enterprises) and Indraprastha Medical Corporation have no direct management commentary in the corpus. Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions.

**ARPOB/ARPP is the dominant KPI, and every chain is engineering it upward via case-mix rather than headline pricing:** Apollo has formally stopped reporting ARPOB (citing investor misreading) and pivoted to ARPP, which rose 11% in Q3 FY26 to ₹1,80,917 on a "5% volume / 4% case mix / 5% pricing" decomposition (Apollo). Narayana credits its ARPOB lift in Bangalore to robotic cardiac surgery and bone marrow transplant volumes, and is pushing patients into higher bed configurations to lift realization while holding occupancy intact (Narayana). Max's international-patient ARPOB, by contrast, was flat — its 25–28% international revenue growth was "entirely volume growth" (Max). Aster's Karnataka cluster ARPP (IP) grew 17% YoY on oncology and neurosciences case-mix shift (Aster).

**Greenfield/brownfield bed additions are unprecedented in scale, but the breakeven math varies sharply by model:** Apollo is committing ₹8,000+ cr for ~4,300 new beds (1,500 in FY27 across Hyderabad/Kolkata/Bangalore/Gurgaon; ~1,400 in key metros at ~25% capacity add), funded internally and breakeven at 50–55% occupancy by FY28 (Apollo). Max is adding ~1,400–1,500 beds, mostly brownfield (~1,000), which Abhay Soi argues should throw off "significantly higher EBITDA margins" than existing business; Dwarka hit EBITDA breakeven in 6 months at 73% occupancy (Max). Aster's pipeline is 4,000+ beds across Trivandrum/Yeshwanthpur/Sarjapur/Hyderabad on 30-year leases (asset-light), with Andhra/Telangana cluster margin ramping 7.9%→13.2% Q1→Q3 FY26 as evidence of absorption (Aster). Fortis operates ~5,900 beds across 34 facilities (Fortis). *[Inference]*: the sector is in a once-in-a-decade capacity cycle; near-term ROCE dilution is the headline risk.

**Oncology and cardiac are the chosen battleground specialties — they're becoming >50% of revenue at the specialty-focused chains:** Narayana explicitly guides oncology + cardiac to exceed half of revenue (cardiac at one-third today, oncology +20% ahead). Apollo's ARPP lift is "a combination of higher complexity cases across oncology and surgical specialties" and its greenfield beds are skewed to oncology, robotics, and high-acuity care. Aster's ARPP growth is driven "mainly [by] Oncology and Neurosciences." Max is installing radiation oncology bunkers at Lucknow and eyeing oncology as a margin lever at acquired units.

**Robotic and minimally invasive surgery is the shared clinical-tech lever — driving ALOS compression, throughput, and ARPOB simultaneously:** Apollo compressed ALOS from 3.33 to 3.19 days (-3.3%) via robotics, MIS, and standardized pathways, lifting bed turnover on a higher-acuity mix. Narayana did ~200 robotic cardiac surgeries in a quarter (97 in September alone) — "the largest volume of robotic cardiac surgery in the country, largely from our Bangalore unit" — and ties this directly to higher realizations and margins. Fortis posted 72% growth in robotic surgeries in FY25. Narayana's Viren Shetty cautions the unit economics are initially bruising: robotic procedures are done "nearly at break-even or sometimes at a loss" during the training ramp.

**CGHS rate revisions (effective Oct 13, late Sept) are a sector-wide positive — but the magnitude differs by payer exposure:** Max Healthcare quantifies the benefit at "over ₹200 crore once fully implemented" (Abhay Soi). Narayana expects ~30% uplift on packaged services and ~12% on non-packaged (R. Venkatesh). Both chains, plus Dr Lal PathLabs in diagnostics, flag CGHS as a material tailwind this fiscal. Separately, Max has "permanently resolved" pricing with three insurance companies (including forward revisions) and is renegotiating GIPSA — signaling that private insurance rate negotiations are the next leg of payer-mix optimization.

**Medical value travel is structurally important (~8% of revenue at Fortis) but geopolitically fragile in the near term:** Fortis's international patient revenue grew 17% last year to ~8% of total, but management warns "there may not be similar growth this year" on geopolitics (Dr. Ashutosh Raghuvanshi). Max grew international patient revenue 25–28% YoY (₹202–353 cr) despite Bangladesh and Yemen unrest — but the gain was volume, not ARPOB. Apollo quantified the Bangladesh drag alone at ~₹100 cr revenue (~1.5% of hospital revenue) and ~50 bps of EBITDA for the full year (K. Akhileswaran). Aster and HCG are positioning for the next medical-tourism wave but the commentary is thinner.

**Payer mix is being deliberately optimized toward self-pay and insurance, away from government schemes — to defend cash flow and pricing power:** Apollo's payor mix is 85% self-pay + insurance, which management frames as shielding it from government pricing pressure while supporting high-end Cardiology/Oncology positioning (Apollo). Narayana is running explicit "payer mix optimization initiatives" alongside its transformation program, pushing patients up the bed-tier ladder (Narayana). Max's insurance renegotiations (including GIPSA) are a continuation of the same theme. *[Inference]*: government-scheme dependence is the structural margin headwind the listed chains are actively shrinking.

**Apollo's HealthCo demerger is the sector's most consequential corporate-action story — it reframes how the hospital pure-play will be valued:** The NCLT-convened shareholder meeting (June 24) is on track for Q4 FY27 completion, with HealthCo targeted at a ₹25,000 cr revenue run-rate and 7% EBITDA margin. Apollo HealthCo EBITDA more than doubled YoY (₹57→₹128 cr), digital cash losses fell to a record-low ₹29 cr, and cash EBITDA breakeven slipped one quarter to Q1 FY27 on an insurance-recognition mismatch. No other hospital chain in the coverage set is executing a comparable restructuring.

> "This growth was driven by a well-balanced mix: 5% from volume growth, 4% from case mix, and the remaining 5% from pricing. Surgical volumes grew by 6% during the quarter, supported by our continued focus on complex specialties."
> — Sunita Reddy, Managing Director, Apollo Hospitals

> "You are seeing these types of numbers because of robotic cardiac surgery and bone marrow transplants; these are very large numbers here. In the last few quarters, we have performed the largest volume of robotic cardiac surgery in the country, largely from our Bangalore unit."
> — Company Management, Narayana Hrudayalaya

> "Brownfields normally give you higher EBITDA margins, right, in percentage terms as well as everything else because your fixed cost is already incurred... even if you are sort of occupying those beds with institutional business or whatever else, even then they give you higher EBITDA per bed as well as EBITDA margins below."
> — Abhay Soi, Chairman and Managing Director, Max Healthcare

## Newsletter synthesis — Packaging (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across laminated tubes (EPL), flexible films (Uflex, SRF), rigid plastics (All Time Plastics, Mold-Tek Packaging, Bai-Kakaji Polymers), and prepress/flexography (Creative Graphics Solutions). Sources span The Chatter and Points & Figures editions.

**BOPET/BOPP film markets under stress from Chinese imports even as domestic demand stays firm:** Uflex flagged that BOPET imports into India surged ~120% YoY and BOPP imports ~100% in H1 FY26, with a fire at a major western-India BOPP plant tightening domestic supply; SRF corroborated the film-cycle turn, noting "some price improvement in BOPET from China" as Chinese competitors pull back irrational pricing. Uflex expects BOPP shortages to persist until new capacity arrives while BOPET importers "have burnt their hands," suggesting an asymmetric recovery across the two film formats.

**Raw material cost pass-through is the operating model, not a margin event:** EPL's management framed polymer cost inflation as routine — "cost inflation passed through to our customers… we do not see a big disruption in either supply or margins." All Time Plastics extends the same logic to tariffs, noting customers (not the converter) decide how much cost to absorb vs. pass to end consumers, because mold dependency makes switching suppliers impractical overnight. This indicates packaging converters across laminate tubes and rigid plastic operate on pass-through contracts rather than absorbing polymer volatility.

**Emerging-markets consolidation is reshaping the laminate tube leader:** EPL's Indovida merger creates a ~$1bn revenue, ~₹8,300cr / ₹1,750cr EBITDA platform with ~75% revenue from emerging markets (Asia, Africa, Latin America) — growing ~1.5–2.5x faster than developed markets across both laminated tubes and rigid PET. The deal combines EPL's flexible/tube franchise with Indovida's rigid PET/bottle/preform capability, giving reciprocal market access (EPL enters Vietnam; Indovida enters India, China, LATAM) and positions the merged entity among the largest EM-focused packaging players with 20%+ EBITDA margins.

**Sustainability regulation (EPR / recycled content) is a live catalyst, not a distant risk — but timelines are slipping:** Mold-Tek Packaging flagged mandatory recycled content (RCP) in paint packaging stepping up from ~20% to 30% shortly, with lubes following. EPL/Indovida are leaning into recyclable tubes and rPET feedstock as a differentiator. Yet Uflex disclosed the government deferred FY26 EPR recycled-content liabilities over three years due to insufficient recycling capacity — meaning the regulatory stick is real but the compliance window is being stretched, creating a near-term cushion and a medium-term inevitability.

**Pharma packaging is the explicit growth vector for rigid plastic and prepress players:** Mold-Tek Packaging's pharma division crossed break-even in Q4 (revenue jumping from ₹3.3cr in Q3 to ₹6.7cr in Q4) with management guiding to 2.5–3x growth in FY25 and adding five injection molding machines; Creative Graphics Solutions is "focusing totally on packaging and flexographic business" with an "aggressive growth plan" to lead pharma packaging, accepting 90-day pharma debtor cycles as the price of larger order sizes. This cross-cuts rigid containers (Mold-Tek) and flexo plates/premedia (CGS) — pharma is pulling both.

**Beverage, bottled water, and e-commerce are the structural volume engines for PET/rigid/flexible:** Bai-Kakaji Polymers' TAM data shows India's packaging market doubling from ~₹3.6L cr (2025) to ~₹7.7L cr (2035), with flexible packaging the fastest segment (+130%) and bottled water growing ~12% CAGR to ~₹2.5L cr by 2034 — directly pulling PET preform and closure demand. Uflex's Cues-in-the-Clutter deck adds the per-capita polymer underpenetration frame (India ~15 kg vs. China/US, implying 4–7x headroom) and notes rural FMCG has outpaced urban for seven straight quarters, a supportive demand backdrop for converters.

**Capacity expansion is underway across formats, financed conservatively:** All Time Plastics is lifting plastic processing capacity from 33,000 MT to 52,500 MT (Khatalwada plant + prospectus capex); Uflex commissioned extended aseptic packaging capacity to 12 billion packs/year (online from October) after hitting record H1 aseptic volumes; Mold-Tek is adding injection molding machines for pharma. Capex is being added into a cyclical downturn — Uflex's CFO explicitly compared the strategy to "SIP" investing, building capacity "when there is no one else investing… when the cycle changes, you will have the gains."

> "India is witnessing much higher level of both BOPET and BOPP imports. Giving you a perspective in H1 FY'26, the BOPET imports in India are up about 120% YOY and BOPP imports are up about 100% over the H1 FY'25."
> — Rajesh Bhatia, Group President & Chief Financial Officer, Uflex

> "Our first priority is to secure supply; our customers are keen that we avoid any supply dislocation. Second, while raw material costs are rising, our model is clear: cost inflation passed through to our customers… We do not see a big disruption in either supply or margins."
> — Management, EPL

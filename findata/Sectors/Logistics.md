The Logistics sector comprises companies involved in movement, storage, and management of goods and services. This sector includes transportation, warehousing, supply chain management, and related services.

## Sub-Sectors

### Transportation
- **Road Transport**: Trucking and fleet management
- **Rail Transport**: Railway freight and passenger services
- **Air Transport**: Cargo and passenger airlines
- **Sea Transport**: Shipping and port operations

### Supply Chain Services
- **Warehousing**: Storage and inventory management
- **Freight Forwarding**: Cargo consolidation and distribution
- **Third-Party Logistics (3PL)**: Outsourced logistics services

### Specialized Logistics
- **Cold Chain**: Temperature-controlled transportation
- **Project Logistics**: Logistics for large projects
- **E-commerce Logistics**: Last-mile delivery services

## Market Characteristics

### Economic Sensitivity
- **Fuel Price Dependency**: Highly sensitive to fuel costs
- **Infrastructure Dependency**: Quality of roads, ports, and airports
- **Trade Volume Sensitivity**: Linked to import/export activity

### Key Trends
- **Digitalization**: Technology adoption for efficiency and visibility
- **Sustainability**: Focus on green logistics and carbon footprint reduction
- **Integration**: Consolidation and end-to-end solutions

## Investment Considerations

### Asset Intensity
- **Capital Requirements**: Significant investment in vehicles, equipment, and facilities
- **Working Capital**: Substantial needs for operations and growth

### Risk Factors
- **Competition**: Intense competition and pricing pressure
- **Regulatory Environment**: Compliance costs and operational constraints
- **Geopolitical Impact**: Trade policies and border issues

## Industry Highlights

### Market Overview
India's logistics sector is experiencing rapid growth driven by e-commerce expansion, infrastructure development, and government initiatives like the PM Gati Shakti master plan.

### Key Trends (Industry)
- **Cold Chain Expansion:** Growing demand for temperature-controlled logistics, especially in pharmaceuticals and food processing
- **Technology Integration:** Adoption of IoT, AI, and blockchain for supply chain visibility and optimization
- **Infrastructure Development:** Major investments in warehousing, last-mile delivery, and multimodal transportation

### Growth Drivers
- E-commerce boom driving last-mile delivery requirements
- Government focus on logistics infrastructure and efficiency
- Rising demand for cold chain solutions in healthcare and food sectors
- Digital transformation of traditional logistics operations

## Newsletter synthesis — Logistics (multi-edition)

THEMES:

- **Express parcel consolidation is the defining story of FY26, with pricing discipline finally returning.** Delhivery's Ecom Express acquisition has made it >100% of the industry profit pool (Sahil Barua, MD & CEO) and pushed its share of the outsourced parcel market to ~27–30% (and "well over half" ex-Amazon/Flipkart self-logistics and Valmo). Allcargo's Ketan Kulkarni reports its Express arm delivered the "highest ever quarter" and was "the only Express company in the top 5 to grow market share in Q2 over Q1." Both managements argue irrational below-cost pricing is finally attriting — Barua notes competitors "have sort of become more disciplined," and Allcargo attributes share gains to discipline rather than discounting.

- **E-commerce / quick-commerce volume ramp is driving warehousing, last-mile and sortation capex across the board.** TCI's Vineet Agarwal calls quick commerce "the flavor of the day… volumes there have been tremendous" and flags rapid warehouse build-out by clients; TCI itself is adding a dedicated e-commerce hub in Delhi (Points & Figures — Capital at Work). TCI Express's Mukti Lal cites festive-season strength "particularly in electronics and lifestyle" and quick-commerce demand from dark stores. Shadowfax is launching 80–100 last-mile facilities per month (Abhishek Bansal, MD & CEO) and entering white goods in FY27. CONCOR's Sanjay Swarup ties volume guidance (20% domestic) to first/last-mile and multimodal build-out.

- **B2B recovery remains uneven — express/3PL is recovering faster than freight forwarding and bulk logistics.** TCI Express flags recovery in "kitchenware, pharma, lifestyle, and engineering" segments on GST cuts and a good monsoon (Mukti Lal). TCI's Agarwal describes a "weak summer" with MSMEs and capital goods sluggish, but post-Diwali restocking and auto-sector GST cuts reviving volumes. TVS Supply Chain Solutions' Ravi Viswanathan is blunt that Global Forwarding Solutions "continues to operate at structurally lower margins" hit by "US tariffs and container availability" — a direct B2B freight headwind the asset-light express players are not facing.

- **3PL contract wins are widening the gap between integrated players and point-solution providers.** TVS SCS finalized a three-year, ₹1,000-crore UK-wide storage-and-distribution mandate with a Fortune 500 British retailer (Ravi Viswanathan, MD) — evidence Indian 3PLs are winning global mandates. Shadowfax onboarded a previously-restricted large marketplace client post-IPO, with revenue benefit expected in subsequent quarters (Abhishek Bansal). Delhivery reports Ecom Express integration costs tracking "materially lower than the ₹300 crores originally forecasted" and retention "well, well, well over" the 30% assumption (Vani Venkatesh, CBO; Sahil Barua) — a clean contract win/absorption story.

- **Multimodal shift (rail/coastal) is a real volume lever for rail-exposed players, but road parcel operators dismiss the threat to their core.** CONCOR is the clearest beneficiary — rolling out "a liberalized policy for cabotage movement and DPD" (Sanjay Swarup), signing an Adani Cement MoU to shift bulk cement road-to-rail via dedicated container rakes (Points & Figures — What the Slides Don't Say), and targeting market share back to 65–70% by FY2029 via "multi modal logistic paths." TCI's Agarwal says "multimodal is growing a lot… container transportation across sea or rail and road has started to pick up," and has ordered two new ships. Contrast: VRL's Sunil Nalavadi dismisses the Dedicated Freight Corridor — "DFC is directly related to full truckloads. The commodity mix we carry is not directly linked with railway services, so the impact of DFC will not be significant on our volumes." The modal shift is segment-specific: bulk/container freight is moving rail/coastal, fragmented LTL parcel is not.

- **Capex pipelines are bifurcating: rail/container and warehousing capex is accelerating; express parcel sortation capex is being deliberately deferred.** CONCOR's Board raised the FY26 capex budget 23% to ₹1,060 crore (₹717 crore already spent) and ties it to a FY2029 vision of ₹15,000 crore top line / 10 million TEUs (Sanjay Swarup). TCI is doubling its decennial capex — "the last 10 years we did about 1,000 crores… the next four years we are going to do 1,000 crores," funded from internal accruals (Vineet Agarwal). VRL guides ₹300–350 crore annually (~₹100–150 cr vehicles, ₹200+ cr land/buildings) on owned hubs (Sunil Nalavadi). On the other side, Delhivery's Amit Agarwal (CFO) says capex on automation equipment "should be minimal" over 2–3 years because the Ecom Express deal added ~35 million monthly sort capacity; TCI Express has cut its 5-year capex target from ₹500 cr to ₹400 cr (Points & Figures — Capital at Work); Shadowfax's Abhishek Bansal says capex spikes to ~4% of revenue this year but normalizes to 2.8–3%.

- **Fleet ownership strategy is splitting between asset-heavy moats and deliberately asset-light models.** VRL's Sunil Nalavadi defends the owned-fleet, on-role-driver model as a moat supporting >21% EBITDA margins and is adding 500 owned trucks (400 by December). Shadowfax's Abhishek Bansal takes the opposite stance: "What we don't intend to do is buy trucks right now… Having trucks is something that creates poor ROCE business in a case like ours," preferring India's third-party truck supply. TCI Express's Mukti Lal confirms a fully-outsourced truck model — "We don't own trucks - our supply is fully outsourced" — noting the GST cut on trucks (28%→18%) has "no impact on our costs." TCI (parent) is the outlier adding ships. The split tracks business model: LTL/parcel density players own; crowdsourced/asset-light express platforms stay light.

- **Diesel/fuel cost management is a live margin lever, with pass-through mechanisms diverging by player scale.** VRL's Sunil Nalavadi details selective route-level rate tweaks rather than blanket hikes — "we identified those routes and made rate tweaks where required" — and reports a 300 bps fuel-cost-as-%-of-revenue reduction (29%→~26%) via bulk procurement (31%→42% of volume) and direct refinery purchases. TCI Express's Mukti Lal frames margin recovery differently — last year margins fell 200 bps, but "higher volumes and better truck utilization should support recovery" as the new air/rail networks ramp. Allcargo's Kulkarni cites "robust domestic demand, sustained infrastructure investments and ongoing policy reforms" as macro tailwinds express-Logistics margins.

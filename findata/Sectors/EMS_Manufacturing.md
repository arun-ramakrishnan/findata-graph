## Newsletter synthesis — EMS_Manufacturing (multi-edition)

A cross-edition synthesis of management commentary across consumer-electronics EMS (Dixon, Amber, PG Electroplast, Virtuoso), industrial/power EMS (Transformers & Rectifiers, Shilchar, Yash Highvoltage, Supreme Power, Quality Power, Atlanta, Rulka), defense/aerospace EMS (Astra Microwave, Centum, Data Patterns, TechEra), and ER&D/OSAT (Kaynes, CyientDLM, Quadrant Future Tek). Sources span The Chatter, Points & Figures and Plotlines editions.

**PLI scheme acting as a structural tailwind, but companies now emphasizing "depth" over headline capacity:** Dixon is expanding its MSIPS-beneficiary camera-module subsidiary Qutek from 70M to 180-190M units and is "supported by strong government policy tailwinds on server manufacturing and backward integration." Kaynes thanked the Ministry of Finance for basic-customs-duty concessions on display/camera-module components, calling it "really helpful" and predicting cash-flow improvement. Centum explicitly frames India's EMS share as rising from 3.8% → 7.7% of the global market, with defence production growing at ~18-19% CAGR "driven by Make in India and indigenization." PG Electroplast's compressor plant remains contingent on "government clearance."

**Backward integration is the dominant margin lever beyond mix:** Dixon (Qutek camera modules, display JV with capacity for 24M mobile + 2.4M automotive/IT displays, "mid-teen double digit margins" targeted at 80-90% utilization), Transformers & Rectifiers ("backward integration journey is well on course... long-lead items have already been placed"), Kaynes (OSAT in Sanand, ₹3,400 cr capex), Virtuoso Optoelectronics (60% of AC components in-house via VCPL compressors / VPPL polymers), Amber Enterprises (PCB consolidation via Ascent + Shogini — "Now Amber has become the largest PCB company"), PG Electroplast (washing-machine capacity to >2M units, compressor project). Centum reframes backward integration as ESDM transformation: "a strategic shift from being a pure EMS company to becoming an integrated ESDM."

**Vertical diversification into EV, aerospace, defense, rail, industrial — away from commoditized consumer assembly:** Dixon is working "with a leading global management consulting firm to design a... roadmap to build a scaled, specialty, high-margin ESDM business, including M&A opportunities focused on the aerospace, defense, automotive, medical, and industrial verticals." Cyient DLM ("strong growth in automotive, industrial, and medical... central to our long-term strategy"), Kaynes ("particularly strong traction from new clients in the electric vehicle industries and aerospace sector, industrial sector, rail sector"), TechEra (private jet tooling MOU, IAF direct supply, L&T bullet components), Astra Microwave (QRSAM ₹1,800-2,000 cr, SU-30 upgrades, "3 new radars" of 100% in-house design), Centum (Sukhoi-30 development order — ₹10 cr development, ~₹1,000 cr platform opportunity), Data Patterns (Precision Approach Radar delivered to Air Force/Navy), Quadrant Future Tek (KAVACH rail signalling, ₹8,287 Mn order book).

**Geopolitical realignment + "China+1" is pulling global OEMs into Indian EMS supply chains:** Kaynes ("big push from automobile giants, Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers to manufacture in India... over the next three to five years, India's manufacturing capability, component availability and technical knowledge will improve significantly"), Cyient DLM ("Geopolitical realignments are reshaping global supply chains. OEMs are actively de-risking footprints and India is emerging as a credible, scalable alternative"), Amber (sublease with Oppo India; 8M → 13-15M mobile unit ramp), Foxconn ("Local for Local" strategy, 300% QoQ AI server growth, EV industry "Outsourcing Breaking Point"). Yash Highvoltage notes "250+ transformer manufacturers today are almost doubling up their capacity" while only 12-13 bushing suppliers exist globally.

**Capex pipeline is broadening — from sustaining to growth-led, with capacity nearly doubling in power-equipment niches:** Transformers & Rectifiers (Changodar from Q2, Moraiya next — capacity from 40,000 → 75,000 MVA), Shilchar (Gavasad expansion +6,500 MVA → 14,000 MVA by April 2027, ₹90 cr via internal accruals), Yash Highvoltage (greenfield scaling 245 KV → 550 KV class), Kaynes (₹3,400 cr OSAT capex, half spent by FY27), PG Electroplast (capex cut from ₹800-900 cr to ₹700-750 cr but land/buildings not held back; washing-machine capacity to >2M units), Amber (asset-light sublease structure with "minimal" capex for Oppo collaboration). Dixon's display line trials begin Q3, commercial Q4.

**JV / partnership with global OEMs is the preferred market-entry vehicle, especially in defense and power:** Dixon ("in discussion with our JV partner to participate in the fast-growing server opportunity"), Yash Highvoltage (50-50 JV with Quality Power to revitalize Sukrut brand — "grow at least 8 to 10 times the Sukrut topline in next 5-6 years"), Quality Power (strategic partnership with Hyosung for Gas Insulated Instrument Transformer technology), Data Patterns ("joint development of radars... where this will not only address requirements in India, but also globally. We can build systems together"), Astra Microwave (JV ARC targeting three single-party tenders totaling ~US$100M by March 2026 with "100% probability"), Centum ("working with some of these startups in different ways... we will be the prime contractor").

**Margin discipline over volume — selective order intake and price-variation clauses to protect against input-cost and FX volatility:** Transformers & Rectifiers ("deliberately not taking orders currently... we want to limit our exposure to no more than 18 to 24 months... as long as we are booked for the next 24 months, we do not want to take any further orders"), Supreme Power (price-variation clause with no ceiling on government orders; "windmill transformers not prioritised" due to low margins), Yash Highvoltage ("we might still have option to select which orders we want to do... it is not necessary that we would be reducing our prices"), Kaynes (memory is only 2-3% of BOM; cost increases are "passed through to customers"), Shilchar (50% US tariff absorbed by customers because "they don't have much of a choice").

**Working-capital intensity rising as order books scale — flagged as a temporary friction, not a structural issue:** Kaynes (WC days spiked to 132 post-August Electronics acquisition; targeting ~70 days normalized), Astra Microwave ("the industry is capital intensive... as your sales grow your receivables will keep growing"), PG Electroplast (close to ₹1,000 cr additional inventory; ₹20 cr incremental financing costs), Data Patterns ("transitory stage... will improve in next two to three years"), Supreme Power (WC at 20-25% of revenue, scaling with ₹300 cr target).

> "Customers are already looking at India and there is a big push from automobile giants, Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers to manufacture in India. Over the next three to five years, India's manufacturing capability, component availability and technical knowledge will improve significantly."
> — Ramesh Kunhikannan, Executive Vice Chairman, Kaynes Technology

> "In phase one, we are setting up a capacity for 24 million mobile displays and 2.4 million automotive and IT product displays... Once we achieve 80-90% utilization, we target revenue of almost 5,500 crores to 6,000 crores with mid-teen double digit margins."
> — Atul Lall, Vice Chairman and Managing Director, Dixon Technologies

> "We are deliberately not taking orders currently because, as we previously mentioned, we want to limit our exposure to no more than 18 to 24 months. We want to be very selective in which orders we take in terms of delivery and pricing. As long as we are booked for the next 24 months, we do not want to take any further orders."
> — Satyen Mamtora, Managing Director and CEO, Transformers & Rectifiers (India)

> "Geopolitical realignments are reshaping global supply chains. OEMs are actively de-risking footprints and India is emerging as a credible, scalable alternative. This plays strongly to our strengths."
> — Rajendra Velagapudi, Managing Director and CEO, Cyient DLM

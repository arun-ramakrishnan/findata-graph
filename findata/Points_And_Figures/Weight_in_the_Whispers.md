# Balanced Nutrition: Shifting from Urea-Heavy to True NPK Farming

India’s nutrient use is skewed to nitrogen – driving a structural push toward NPK &amp; soil health solutions.

![[images/Weight_in_the_Whispers_p15_img25.jpeg]]

- Overuse of urea and underuse of P &amp; K are linked to:
- Soil nutrient mining &amp; degradation
- Declining marginal response to fertiliser
- Higher vulnerability to climate shocks &amp; yield volatility

- Regulatory &amp; Market response:
- Government schemes (Soil Health Cards, PM-PRANAM, Nano fertilisers, ONOF “Bharat NPK”) explicitly push farmers toward balanced NPK use and crop-specific recommendations.
- NPK complexes and SSP growing faster than overall fertiliser market (NP/NPK up ~9.9% in 2023–24).

Positioning of Krishana Phoschem Ltds:

- From Product to Crop Nutrient Solution:
- KRISHANA’s portfolio in SSP, NPK complexes, WSFs, fortified &amp; sulphur/secondary nutrient products is directly mapped to the P, K, S, and micronutrient gaps in Indian soils.
- Structural Demand Driver:
- As India’s NPK ratio normalizes towards 4:2:1, incremental growth must come from P &amp; K-rich grades, not urea – structurally lifting the NPK/SSP/WSF.
- Farmer &amp; Policy Aligned:
- Balanced nutrition improves yield, nutrient-use efficiency and income per hectare – making Krishana’s offerings highly aligned with both government soil-health goals and farmer economics.

“Shift from ‘more fertilizer’ to ‘right fertilizer mix’ is structurally NPK-positive.”

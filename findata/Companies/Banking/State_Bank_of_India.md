# State Bank of India

## Company Overview

State Bank of India (SBI) is India's largest public sector bank, established in 1806 as the Bank of Calcutta and renamed as State Bank of India in 1955. A government-owned entity with over 200 years of banking heritage, SBI operates 24,000+ branches and 59,000+ ATMs across India, providing retail banking, corporate banking, treasury operations, and international banking across 14 countries.

### Key Signals (Q3 FY26)
- **Credit growth guidance raised**: Revised up from 12–14% to 13–15% for the current quarter.
  > "Regarding credit growth, we had previously given guidance of 12-14%. We are revising upwards to 13-15% for the current quarter." — SBI Management Team
- **CVE income secular growth**: Cross-selling of life insurance, mutual funds; enhanced product breadth via counters and YONO.
  > "There is a secular movement in Customer Value Enhancement (CVE) income which is basically cross-selling income." — SBI Management Team
- **Data-center financing + renewables**: Active in data-center financing; green portfolio at INR 1 lakh cr (largely renewable energy).
  > "We are active in data center financing... Renewable energy is a major focus for us; our green portfolio has reached 1 lakh crores." — SBI Management Team
- **Corporate banking ecosystem model**: 13%+ corporate credit growth in Q3 without compromising margins; moved from "corporate lending to corporate banking" (22 non-funding areas).
  > "In Q3, we had over 13% corporate credit growth without compromising margins... We have moved from corporate lending to corporate banking." — SBI Management Team
- **International expansion = wholesale focus**: Leveraging existing geographies (e.g., New York = largest operation) for corporate/MSME trade-deal funding; no aggressive EU branch expansion.
  > "In these jurisdictions, we act primarily as wholesale bankers. Trade deals will help us in corporate and MSME funding." — SBI Management Team
- **Corporate bond market tailwind**: RBI's Partial Credit Enhancement to let lower-rated (A/AA) corporates access the bond market; SBI will participate while prioritizing loan funding.
  > "The new allowance for Partial Credit Enhancement by the RBI will allow lower-rated corporates (A or AA) to access the bond market, which is currently dominated by AAA companies." — SBI Management Team

## The Chatter — SBI Delhivery Titan

**Credit growth guidance 14-15%, anchored to nominal GDP:** 18% Q1 YoY credit growth benefited from a weak base; sustainable growth pegged at 2-3% above ~12-12.5% nominal GDP.

**Corporate pipeline exceeds ₹9 lakh crores:** Strong visibility across term loans, undisbursed sanctions, and working-capital facilities; M&A financing opening new segments (e.g. software companies borrowing for acquisitions).

**Full-year NIM guidance maintained at 3%:** Despite FCNR(B) mobilisation and changing liquidity, management held to its full-year domestic NIM target; unwilling to chase expensive wholesale deposits.

**Retail franchise resilient:** Retail term deposits growing 14%, savings bank base of ₹17.5 lakh crore growing 10% — an exception in an industry struggling with CASA.

**Gold loan franchise at ₹3.1 trillion:** Personal gold loans ₹1.25T + agricultural ₹1.85T; ROE-accretive with near-zero risk weight and no capital allocation requirement.

> "18% credit growth has to be viewed in the context of the base effect. Q1 of the previous year was a muted quarter... We believe nominal GDP may be around 12-12.5 and SBI has always grown 2-3% more than that. That is why we have given credit growth guidance of 14-15%."
> — C. S. Setty, Chairman

# Gujarat Energy

> Amalgamated entity formed from the merger of **Gujarat Gas Limited** (city gas distribution) and **Gujarat State Petronet Limited** (GSPL, natural gas transmission), renamed to **Gujarat Energy Limited** in May 2026.

## Company Overview

Gujarat Energy Limited (formerly Gujarat Gas Limited), incorporated in 2012 and headquartered in Gandhinagar, is a natural gas distribution and transmission company. It operates approximately 42,600 km of pipeline network and 828 CNG stations, supplying piped natural gas (PNG) to ~22.6 lakh households, ~15,600 commercial customers, and ~4,400 industrial customers. The amalgamation with Gujarat State Petronet (GSPL) consolidates gas transmission infrastructure with city gas distribution, creating an integrated gas utility. The company is also engaged in wind power generation and a green hydrogen blending pilot, and provides IT services for Gujarat e-governance. It operates across Gujarat, Maharashtra, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, and Daman & Diu.

## Business Segments

- **Natural Gas Distribution**: ~42,600 km pipeline network delivering gas across multiple Indian states.
- **Natural Gas Transmission (legacy GSPL)**: Gas transmission utility operating extensive pipeline infrastructure connecting gas sources to demand centers; critical infrastructure for Gujarat's industrial development and energy security.
- **CNG Retail**: 828 CNG stations serving automotive/transport demand.
- **PNG — Domestic**: Piped gas to ~22.6 lakh households.
- **PNG — Commercial & Industrial**: Supply to ~15,600 commercial and ~4,400 industrial customers.
- **Renewables & New Energy**: Wind power generation and green hydrogen blending pilot.
- **IT / e-Governance Services**: IT support for Gujarat state e-governance initiatives.
- **Geographic Footprint**: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu.

## Key Insights (from Edition #24, Mar 2026)
- Industrial dominance makes Gujarat Gas's volumes a proxy for manufacturing cycles and fuel-switching economics; this exposure links margins directly to the price gap between gas and alternative fuels.
- The company is expanding its CBG (Compressed Bio-Gas) offtake strategy — signing 27 bio-gas supplier agreements (8 new in Q3 FY26 alone) across its authorised areas — which helps decouple supply from volatile global pricing and currency risks while ensuring compliance with green energy mandates. GGL was amongst the first CGD companies to offtake CBG into its NG pipeline network and commissioned India's first CBG off-take facility via steel pipeline at Batala, Gurdaspur.
- A 12 MW captive solar project (under the capex model) reduces fixed utility costs for company-owned CNG stations, CGS and offices — protecting margins from rising grid tariffs as the retail network expands geographically.
- ESG initiatives also include tree plantation (4,600+ saplings in FY26) and measurable CO2 emission reductions (~56 lakh kg/day via PNG substitution for coal; ~21 lakh kg/day via CNG substitution for petrol/diesel).

## The Chatter — Q4FY26 (Havells, Ambuja, Cipla & More)

*Pre-merger Gujarat Gas commentary (the standalone CGD entity prior to the May 2026 amalgamation into Gujarat Energy).*

**Corporate restructuring:** The merger process was expected to be finalized by April 2026 (MCA queries addressed; final hearing expected mid-February, with completion and share allotment by end of April).

**Industrial (Morbi) volumes:** Suffered because propane was considerably cheaper than GGL's natural gas, causing volume contraction in the industrial segment. To counter this, GGL planned to actively offer propane as an alternative fuel solution (leveraging parent GSPC's identified storage locations and securing unloading capacity) to retain customers and market share.

**CNG growth:** CNG sales rose 11% YoY — Gujarat +9%, areas outside Gujarat +22%, underscoring geographic diversification success. Target: expand fuel retail footprint to over 1,000 CNG stations within 2-3 years.

**Sourcing — APM gas shortfall:** Low-cost domestic (APM) gas allocation shortfall increased from 45% to 51% of total requirement (CNG shortfall at 64%), forcing reliance on spot and long-term contracts. Target: secure up to 70% of supply through long-term contracts to hedge against price spikes.

**LNG market outlook:** A significant supply glut was projected over the next 2-3 years from new liquefaction capacities (particularly US and Qatar), putting downward pressure on spot prices in Europe and Asia — favorable for India's long-term demand.

**Open access:** Legal proceedings on third-party access to gas networks were ongoing but stalled (order reserved) — continued legal protection prevented immediate competitive disruption to the distribution monopoly.

# Saudi Aramco

## Company Overview

Saudi Aramco (officially the Saudi Arabian Oil Company) is the world's largest oil-producing company and one of the top-ranked global corporations by revenue, headquartered in Dhahran, Saudi Arabia. It manages over 270 billion barrels of crude oil reserves and operates extensive upstream, downstream, and petrochemical businesses globally, organized into Upstream and Downstream segments.

- **Founded:** 1933 | **Headquarters:** Dhahran, Saudi Arabia
- **Listed:** Saudi Tadawul (2222.SR)
- **CEO & President:** Amin H. Nasser
- **Segments:** Upstream (exploration, production, sale of crude, condensate, gas, NGLs) and Downstream (chemicals, refining, petrochemicals, retail, lubricants, power)

- **East-West Pipeline reroute**: Aramco rapidly ramped up the East-West Pipeline (up to 5 million bpd, mostly for export) to reposition tankers from east to west, expecting to reach capacity within days — constrained mainly by tanker availability; flows already more than double initial levels.
- **Global storage leverage**: Leveraging storage in the Kingdom and abroad (Japan, Korea, Netherlands/Rotterdam) to maintain export continuity and meet customer requirements during the Strait of Hormuz disruption.
- **Customer coordination**: Majority of exports sold FOB; working closely with customers/partners to minimize disruption impact and supportive of geopolitical measures to keep supply flowing.
- **LNG strategy scaling**: Cumulative potential offtake of up to **3.2 million tons by 2030**, including stakes in LNG projects and a 20-year SPA with NextDecade for 1.2 million tons.
- **Capex discipline**: Spending came in at the low end of guidance — a function of timing, not project cuts; major investment program remains on track.
- **Operational flexibility**: Can ramp up production "in days, not weeks" using ample spare capacity; even shut-in wells can return to market in days.
- **Hormuz risk**: A prolonged disruption could remove ~**20% of global supply** (~17–18 million bpd through Hormuz); even with western export ability, ~350 million barrels of disruption exposure.
- **Refining posture**: West Coast refineries fully operational and critical for local + export markets; Eastern refineries being sized to meet Kingdom demand since Hormuz export is constrained.
- **Local sourcing as moat**: >70% of supply chain fulfilled from inside the Kingdom keeps major projects on schedule despite geopolitical disruption.
- **Jafurah gas on track**: Phase One to 650 bcf; Phase Two to a sustainable 2 bcf/day by 2030, drilling more wells each year.

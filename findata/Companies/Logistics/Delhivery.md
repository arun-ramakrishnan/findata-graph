# Delhivery

## Company Overview

Delhivery is a logistics company that provides delivery, supply chain management, and warehousing services to businesses and consumers across India. It is a mid-cap player in the Indian logistics sector with a focus on third-party logistics (3PL) and express parcel services.

## Key Insights (Contours_of_Change)

- **Ecom Express acquisition strengthens market leadership**: Delhivery is the only profitable player in a fragmented 3PL market, and its acquisition of Ecom Express signals inevitable consolidation among loss-making competitors. Management notes Delhivery represents "more than 100% of the profit pool of this industry." (Sahil Barua, MD & CEO)
- **Faster-than-expected volume retention post-acquisition**: Customer volumes are migrating into the Delhivery network earlier than anticipated, with April and May volumes running above March — unusual strength given Q1 is typically soft seasonally. Volume retention assumption (~30% of Ecom Express volumes) is being exceeded organically. (Sahil Barua, MD & CEO)
- **Disciplined capital allocation post-acquisition**: Long-term capex guidance of 3.5%-4% of revenue holds, with minimal spend on automation equipment expected over the next 2-3 years. The Ecom Express deal added roughly 35 million monthly parcel-sorting capacity, reducing the need for new sortation systems. (Amit Agarwal, CFO)
- **Competitive pricing pressure normalizing**: Earlier in-house/self-logistics pricing pressures are now normalizing, with attrition of weak players expected to provide margin tailwinds and market share gains in FY26-27. Self-logistics businesses do not compare favorably to Delhivery's network on cost or service performance. (Sahil Barua, MD & CEO)

## The Chatter — SBI Delhivery Titan

**Freight yield improvement is organic, not seasonal:** 37-paisa yield improvement with only ~6 paisa from fuel pass-through; structural gains from network quality and scale.

**Launching financial services arm for truck partners:** Facilitating fleet financing/insurance through lending partners (not Delhivery's balance sheet) to expand reliable supply capacity.

**Minimum wages a bigger margin threat than fuel:** No contractual pass-through clause for wage hikes (unlike fuel); but management expects industry-wide statutory increases to be passable to customers over time.

**Staying out of quick-commerce last-mile:** Viewing it as a low-margin, eventually-captive service; instead focusing on backend logistics (moving goods to mother warehouses and dark stores).

**Real-time routing software as key moat:** Automated decisions on which packages to accept/route — difficult to replicate even offline, let alone in real time.

> "On PTL yields, this is not a seasonal improvement in yields at all. It is a planned and delivered improvement in yields... we have seen something like a 37-paisa improvement in yield, of which only about 6 paisa is coming from fuel inflation."
> — Sahil Barua, MD & CEO

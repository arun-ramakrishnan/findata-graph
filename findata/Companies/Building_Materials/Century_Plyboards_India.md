# Century Plyboards (India)

## Company Overview
Century Plyboards (India) Limited is a leading Indian wood-panel manufacturer — plywood, laminates and MDF — with multiple plants across India, expanding in high-growth MDF and value-added laminate segments. Plywood capacity running near 100% utilisation; Hoshiarpur facility commissioning (next volume leg), a UP plywood unit next; long-term financial policy: debt below 1.0x EBITDA, ROCE hurdle 20%+.

## The Chatter — IRDAI Chairman, Bajaj Auto, SBI & More

**MDF margins actively recovering:** Q2 demand traction across all segments robust; most raw-material price increases passed through — Q2 margins similar to or slightly better than Q1, with H2 margin expansion expected as input costs ease.

**Asymmetric pass-through as a margin lever:** if crude-derivative prices cool, Century retains part of the savings to expand gross margins rather than passing them through — full pass-through is smaller in Plywood/Laminates; in MDF and Particle Board the retention builds a deliberate cushion.

**Plywood at record levels:** 30%+ growth in Q1; July marked the highest monthly Plywood sales volume in company history at near-100% capacity utilisation — official guidance "will be updated upward when released"; Hoshiarpur commissioning (next month) is the near-term volume unlock.

**Peak debt is working-capital, not distress:** rapid top-line growth (30% Plywood) ties up cash in a 45–60 day working-capital cycle, driving incremental short-term borrowings; deleveraging has begun — long-term debt-to-EBITDA "comfortably below 1.0x" by fiscal year-end.

**Capex downshift funds the deleveraging:** next major expansion is a plywood unit in UP late next year (significantly lower capex than MDF/Particle Board); no new greenfield MDF/PB plants planned — strong operating cash flows drive sequential debt reduction. ROCE expected to cross the internal 20% hurdle within two years (by FY29).

**Laminates exports: timing, not demand:** container constraints defer shipments across quarter boundaries ("27th to the 1st") rather than destroying demand; sequential quarterly improvement expected as logistics normalize.

> "Deleveraging has already begun. By the end of this fiscal year, our long-term debt-to-EBITDA ratio will sit comfortably below 1.0x."
> — Keshav Bhajanka, Executive Director

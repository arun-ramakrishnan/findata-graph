## The Chatter — NSE ONGC SEBI

**Derivatives tightening framed as correction, not threat:** Management says retail-options curbs are a temporary correction phase — derivatives must serve price discovery, hedging and risk management, not speculation — and guides underlying growth potential in the early double digits.

**Revenue mix rebalanced away from weekly options:** Out of every ₹100 operating income, ~₹42 comes from weekly options and ~₹58 from non-weekly sources — monthly options, index futures (~8–9%), monthly stock options (~8–9%), market data, colocation, terminal fees and communications (~12–15%).

**Resilience vs expectations:** When tightening began, the street feared a 30–40% revenue hit; the actual dip was 2–3% before rebounding. Q1 FY27 delivered 8–10% YoY growth across revenue, EBITDA and PAT.

**New growth avenues:** NSE International Exchange at GIFT City IFSC as a global-capital gateway, plus Electronic Gold Receipts (EGRs) to monetise India's estimated 35,000 tonnes of domestic gold reserves and cut physical gold imports.

**Operating leverage + regulation-first listing stance:** Fixed infrastructure/manpower base means volumes expand EBITDA margins; management quantifies an economic beta of ~1.5x–2.0x to India's GDP growth. Subsidiary listings need critical scale plus regulatory comfort (market-infrastructure arms may stay unlisted as public utilities); self-listing is a logical long-term framework, not an active pursuit.

> "Three years ago, when regulatory tightening began, market participants expected our revenues to drop 30% to 40%. In reality, revenue dipped by just 2% to 3% before rebounding. In Q1 FY27, NSE delivered 8% to 10% year-on-year growth across revenue, EBITDA, and PAT."
> — Ashishkumar Chauhan, Managing Director & CEO

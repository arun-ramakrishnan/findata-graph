# SRF

SRF Limited is a diversified industrial conglomerate and a global leader in the manufacturing of fluorochemicals, specialty chemicals, and technical textiles. The company also operates a significant packaging film business providing solutions for the food and industrial sectors.

## The Chatter — Q4FY26 (Havells, Ambuja, Cipla & More)

**Specialty Chemicals — Chinese pricing pressure:** Internal business improvements (product mix, operational efficiencies) are being overshadowed by aggressive, irrational pricing from Chinese competitors. Management expects this is unsustainable and a market correction will come, though timing is uncertain.

> "From our discussions with stakeholders, it is evident that Chinese players are finding it difficult to sustain these price levels, and we believe that this situation is not viable in the long run. When this correction will happen, remains difficult to predict at this stage."
> — Ashish Bharat Ram, Chairman and Managing Director

**Agrochemicals:** Continued deferment in offtake by agro majors, but crop-protection agrochemicals are showing signs of revival. Strong product pipeline gives confidence to finish the year strongly.

**Pharma intermediates — diversification:** Adding a second pharma intermediate plant at INR 180 crore (Dahej site), commissioned in ~8 months. Strategic intent: reduce dependence on the volatile agricultural market. Target: double pharma's contribution in specialty chemicals from ~10% to at least 20%.

**Refrigerant gases:**
- High HFC prices (China quota-led supply restrictions + steady global demand) boosted profitability even in a traditionally lean season.
- Industry transition to next-generation environmentally friendly refrigerants is inevitable; companies with capex- and opex-efficient plants will benefit.
- Baseline production in 2024-2026 will determine future manufacturing quotas.
- Domestic R32 oversupply (~17,000-18,000 tonne demand vs substantially higher capacity) forcing exports.

**Packaging films (BOPET):** Prices improving as Chinese competitors reduce excess supply — should benefit margins in coming months.

**Currency:** Unprecedented rupee depreciation negatively impacted forward hedges (expected to persist a few quarters), though a weak rupee is generally favorable.

## The Chatter — Infosys Adani Power

**Best-ever Q1, films-driven:** Packaging Films posted an exceptional Q1 on supply constraints and geopolitical-driven price spikes; SRF ran its DTA unit at ~100% capacity while competitors went off-stream, thanks to its global sourcing model. Baseline earnings are expected to recalibrate higher even as Q2 normalises.

**Specialty chemicals inflecting:** First meaningful signs of recovery after a prolonged downturn — both volumes and pricing are turning up, though the recovery is gradual/pockets-only rather than broad-based. SRF protected market share through the downturn (rather than maximising margin), which is now paying off as customers raise offtake.

**Refrigerant gases structurally stronger:** Management says "the time for gas has arrived" — the fluorochemicals portfolio is delivering on a plan set in motion earlier, and will remain a big driver rather than a cyclical pop.

**Pharma CDMO ambition:** Stated goal of 20–30% of revenue from pharma by 2030; a growing molecule count and customer base is raising the probability of step-function scale, though still early.

**De-risking the films business:** New capex (metallized/coated products, BOPA, capacitors) is aimed at lifting the value-added mix to reduce dependence on commodity polyester-film cycles.

> "We are starting to see volumes over a little bit through last quarter and this quarter as well. The trend is upward. So that is the first sign, and I think the second piece, which is probably the one that has you more concerned, is around price. At least across key products at this point in time, we are finally starting to see the price trend marginally upwards in the right direction."
> — Sameer Kashyap, President & Chief Financial Officer

# Tata Steel

## Company Overview

Tata Steel is a globally diversified steel producer with integrated operations from mining to marketing. Raw material operations in India and Canada ensure self-sufficiency, while downstream business units in India include Ferro-Alloys, Tubes, Wires, Bearings, and more. It is one of India's largest steel producing companies, with complete value-chain integration and advanced steel production technologies.

### Regional Demand Divergence
- **India:** Apparent steel demand continued to grow, aided by government spending and stimulus measures; India turned net steel exporter in Oct'25; 3-year safeguard duty ranging 12% to 11% announced in Dec'25

- **UK:** Demand remained weak amidst policy mismatch with EU, steel imports, and US tariffs

### Spreads Compressed by Firm Raw Materials
- Global steel prices diverged in the Oct-Dec quarter: US HRC prices crossed $1,000/t (aided by tariffs) while Germany prices moved closer to $750/t; UK prices diverged from EU due to policy differential
- China HRC prices mostly rangebound; exports reached a record high of 119 mn tons in 2025
- Raw material prices firmer — Iron ore above $100/t and Coking coal above $200/t — leading to tight spreads across regions

### India Deliveries — Record Quarter
- India deliveries rose 9% QoQ to a record 6 million tons (first time crossing 6 mn tons in a quarter), led by strong domestic demand across auto, construction, infrastructure, and engineering segments

## The Chatter — Tata Steel, LIC, Vodafone, BPCL & More

**India crude steel +8% on Kalinganagar ramp:** Annual crude steel production and deliveries rose ~8% YoY to ~23 million tons, supported by the successful ramp-up of the 5 MTPA Kalinganagar expansion and downstream facilities.

**UK fixed cost halved but still loss-making:** Fixed cost cut by ~50% from a ~£1 bn base in FY24, but cheap imports and weak demand continue to drive EBITDA losses of ~£98/ton — revised trade frameworks are expected to materially improve conditions.

**Overseas debt slashed:** Overseas debt reduced from ~50% of total debt (2021) to 18% in FY26, with further reduction by FY28 as dollar bonds are repaid; only working capital lines will remain overseas, insulating against INR weakness.

**60 MT India optionality unlocked:** With Kalinganagar, Meramandali, Neelachal, Jamshedpur, plus the announced Maharashtra site, Tata Steel has the optionality to scale to ~60 million tons in India — a flexibility unavailable a decade ago.

**Shift to value-added mix:** Management is deliberately selling less hot-rolled commodity steel in favor of cold-rolled, galvanized, and packaging steel to dampen cyclicality and stabilize margins; volumes expected to be at least 2 MT higher YoY on Kalinganagar ramp.

**Netherlands uncertainty flagged:** A vague regulatory letter on environmental permits triggered a 'material uncertainty' disclosure; management is pushing for a sequential, planned coke-oven transition rather than an abrupt shutdown, and will not commit large Netherlands capex until clarity emerges.

> "Strategically we feel it is not just about steelmaking capacity. The cost of iron ore in India is going up. The value pools will shift. Value pools are not necessarily upstream going forward. Some of those value pools will shift downstream."
> — T. V. Narendran, CEO and Managing Director

## The Chatter — RBI Tata Steel

**Clean-year volume lift:** FY26 is free of major maintenance outages (the next blast-furnace relining at Jamshedpur falls in FY27), so management expects crude steel volumes ~2 million tons higher than the prior year — a clear window for output before the next shutdown.

**Margin compression offset by volume:** India margins will compress in Q2 as steel prices step down ~₹1,500/t versus Q1, but Q2 volumes are set to rise enough that profit in rupee-crore terms should still be higher — volume makes up for the per-ton squeeze.

**Downstream value capture:** Strategy is pivoting toward midstream/downstream (branded Tata Tiscon, Steelium, Astrum) over incremental upstream build; the stickiness of these franchises is what lets India margins hold whether steel prices rise or fall.

**Disciplined balance sheet:** Net debt/equity held at ~2.3, forecast to stay in the 2.3–2.5 range; internal cash generation is expected to fully fund planned capex without fresh borrowing.

**West Asia headwind absorbed:** The Middle East crisis cost ~₹1,200 cr in the quarter, yet numbers held up because of cumulative cost-takeout actions over prior years.

> "We believe that the value capture opportunity lies more in midstream and downstream than in upstream… the stickiness of our margins is reflective of the fact that we have a fairly strong downstream presence and a very good franchise with products like Tata Tiscon, Tata Steelium, Tata Astrum, etc."
> — T. V. Narendran, MD & CEO

# Polycab India

## Company Overview
Polycab India Limited, founded in 1964 and headquartered in Mumbai, is India's largest manufacturer of wires and cables, with a growing presence in Fast Moving Electrical Goods (FMEG) and EPC projects. Its three segments — Wires & Cables, FMEG (fans, lighting, switches, water heaters, solar), and EPC (power transmission/distribution) — make it a play on power infrastructure, electrification, and consumer electrical demand.

## Key Insights (from newsletter)
- Demand led by low- and medium-voltage cables (~50% of industry volume), with strong pull also from high-voltage/EHV cables as renewables and power projects create a "very big opportunity" (Gandharv Tongia, ED & CFO).
- Export push under "Project Spring": targeting exports rising from 6% to 10% of revenue.
- US sales dropped from ~40% of exports to high-teens due to tariff uncertainty, but management expects recovery in coming months; India benefits from relatively lower tariffs vs other Asian exporters.
- Sustained good demand across industries, especially from the transition to renewable energy sources.

## The Chatter — Meesho, Marico, Bajaj & More

**Self-funded growth:** A planned ₹60–80 billion capex over five years is well-covered by a strengthened balance sheet, with net cash rising to ~₹41 billion — limiting reliance on incremental debt.

**Capex concentrated in cables:** ~90% of FY27 capex (per Project Spring) goes into cable & wire capacity, ~5% into backward integration, and only ~3–4% into FMEG — focused spend to defend core leadership.

**EHV entry to cut imports:** Extra-high-voltage cable capacity comes onstream by end of this calendar year, opening tender-based, higher-value projects where ~50% of current domestic consumption is still imported.

**Solar breakout + US rebuild:** The solar products business doubled YoY to become the largest FMEG category, and Polycab has re-established its US distribution network to lift the export mix.

**Inflation fully passed on:** Thanks to backward integration (in-house compounding) and fortnightly price resets, Polycab fully passed through copper/aluminum/PVC moves — though a 60–80% PVC spike plus West-Asia sentiment did dent distributor offtake.

> "EHV is very much on track. Capacity is expected to come on stream by the end of this calendar year. In FY28, we can see some addition in revenue from EHV because it is a tender-based business and we see a ready market. About 50% of domestic consumption today is coming from imports."
> — Shashank Jagani, Head of Strategy and Investor Relations

## The Chatter — Jio Financial, Wipro, Polycab, Piramal & More

**Strategic priorities reaffirmed:** Targeting 1.5x–2x industry growth with progressive profitability improvement; ongoing investments in distribution, product innovation, and brand.

**Solar = primary FMEG growth engine:** Solar category delivered >2x YoY growth — benefits from PM Surya Ghar Yojana, state incentives, and rising renewable adoption.

**Cost-plus pricing in cables:** Pricing is cost-plus — material cost fluctuations pass-through; protects profitability despite copper/aluminium volatility.

**Data centre opportunity:** India's installed data centre base ~1.6 GW, projected to reach 8–18 GW; ~1 MW translates to ~₹3.5 crore of cables (50–60% conventional, balance optical fibre).

**Export diversification:** North America contributes 45–50% of Q1 international turnover, Europe ~20%; added 10 new geographies last year.

**BharatNet fibre secured:** Procurement strength has secured fibre for next 2–3 years of execution — insulated from current high fibre prices.

**Power T&D structural demand:** Every ₹100 spent on T&D translates to 15% cable requirement; transformer companies' order books now 2.5x revenue — strong lead indicator.

> "A ₹100 spend on transmission and distribution translates to a cable requirement of 15%, which is very high. We believe the next five years will be monumental for generation, transmission and distribution combined."

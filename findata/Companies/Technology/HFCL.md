# HFCL

HFCL Limited (formerly Himachal Futuristic Communications Limited) is a New Delhi-based technology company specialising in telecom infrastructure, optical fibre cables and connectivity solutions. Its portfolio spans underground/aerial/microduct/FTTH fibre cables, unlicensed-band backhaul radios, Wi-Fi access points, routers, switches, antennas, 5G products, defence electronics (fuzes, SDRs, electro-optics, radars) and passive networking components. It also offers system integration for telecom, defence and railway networks.

## The Chatter — Adani Power Motilal Oswal

**Growth guidance raised again — to ≥60%:** After guiding 20% and then raising to 40% post-Q1, management now aspires to "grow by a minimum of 60% over the FY26 numbers" on strong AI/data-centre tailwinds.

**Structural, not cyclical, fibre demand:** Of ~750 million global fibre-km demand, 400–450M comes from hyperscaler data centres; defence drone fibre (40–45 km per drone, single-use) adds ~100M fibre-km of consumable demand. Supply is 600M fibre-km and demand is seen reaching 900M by FY29 — the market stays tight despite everyone adding capacity. Management sees a 7–8 year runway (hyperscalers, telecom capex, 6G, defence).

**Realisations re-rated:** A2 fibre for hyperscalers has gone from $7–8 to $25 per fibre-km; 85–90% of revenue is now AI/data-centre hyperscaler business, mostly exports at better margins. Data-centre cables run 864–6,912 fibres vs a 288-fibre max in legacy telecom — a different, richer product; hyperscalers are booking capacity 5–7 years out (up to 10 in some cases).

**Telecom capex is the next leg:** Telcos paused capex for 1–1.5 years betting fibre prices would soften; they haven't. Inquiries from large global telecoms have started, and management expects the next demand phase to come from network densification.

**No Chinese competition in AIDC:** Hyperscalers don't buy Chinese product in this segment, and HFCL's scale/bulk-buying keeps it competitive globally. Lead indicator: a data-centre announcement translates into cable demand 5–6 quarters later.

> "The fibre supplied to hyperscalers is called A2 fibre. It used to trade at around $7–8 per fibre kilometre, and it has now reached $25 per fibre kilometre."
> — Amit Agarwal, Investor Relations

# Tata Consumer Products

**Pricing power as margin hedge:** A balanced mix of stronger-commodity and highly-processed food gives enough headroom to take price increases and mitigate margin impact from input inflation.

**Tea leadership on digital channels:** Market leader in tea on both quick-commerce and e-commerce, indicating successful capture of younger, tech-savvy consumers.

**Benign tea costs:** Tea prices trending in the same ballpark as last year, allowing Tata Consumer to give up pricing to remain competitive and improve earnings predictability.

## The Chatter — IDFC, Tata Consumer, Bank of India, Lal PathLabs & More

**Growth segment >⅓ of India business:** "growth businesses" now contribute >⅓ of the India business (best-ever quarter); Sampann +58% (broad-based volume) and RTD revenue +41%.

**US branded share streak:** US business +7% constant-currency with the seventh consecutive quarter of share gains, offsetting the -10% cc decline in non-branded coffee commodities.

**Sampann margin turnaround:** moved from -5% margin (2020) to ~12%, with 150-200 bps YoY margin expansion this quarter on operating leverage.

**Premium niche over mass oil:** targeting cold-pressed oils (trust-deficit category) with the Tata brand rather than the base edible-oil mass market — driving growth with margins, avoiding low-differentiation price wars.

**Tea inflation watch:** 7-10% input inflation currently, skewed to the lower end of the portfolio; peak Assam cropping season just begun and the buying season is largely still ahead.

**Water white-space:** ~75% availability but only 40-50% real distribution/marketing execution — North and West are broadly white-space geographies for beverages.

> "In Sampann, we moved from a negative 5% margin in 2020 to closing in on 12%. This quarter had 150-200 bps of margin expansion year-over-year. The most critical piece is operating leverage."
> — Sunil D'Souza, Managing Director and CEO

# Swiggy

## Overview

Swiggy is a consumer-first technology company providing a convenient platform for ordering food, groceries, household items, making restaurant reservations, event bookings, product pick-up/drop-off services, and engaging in hyperlocal commerce activities.

The company has built two of India's largest consumer internet categories:
- Food delivery (launched 2014): Operates in 718 cities with annualized GOV of ₹34,000 crore
- Quick commerce via Instamart (launched 2020): Covers 124 cities with ₹28,000 crore in annualized GOV

Swiggy has expanded through strategic acquisitions like Dineout and by launching innovations like Bolt (10-minute food delivery), co-branded credit cards, and gifting solutions, all powered by a modular tech stack that enables fast, low-cost experimentation.

## Key Insights

### Growth Runway
- Only 57 million annual transacting users out of 128 million who have ever used the platform
- Just 18 million monthly active users
- Represents less than half of the total addressable market of 230-250 million online commerce users in India
- Focus on unlocking potential through new use cases, affordability initiatives, and reactivating dormant users

### Q2 FY26 Performance
**Food Delivery:**
- Monthly transacting users: Up 17.2% YoY to 17.2 million
- Gross order value: Up 18.8% to ₹8,542 crore
- Highest double-digit order growth in two years, driven by improved value proposition and higher user engagement
- GC growing consistently at ~18.5% YoY over past four quarters

**Instamart (Quick Commerce):**
- GOV: ₹7,022 crore (up 107.6% YoY)
- Orders: 101 million (up 48.7% YoY)
- Average order value: ₹69 (up 39.7% YoY)
- Balanced growth across volume and basket size, catering to wider purchase missions while deepening wallet share

### Network Effects
Swiggy's platform creates powerful network effects by connecting users, restaurant partners, merchant partners, brand partners, and delivery partners across multiple services (food delivery, quick commerce, dining out, events). More demand attracts more supply, driving higher engagement through advertising, reservations, digital storefronts, delivery services, and the Swiggy One membership program. This flywheel effect strengthens value proposition for all stakeholders and fuels sustainable growth.

### Competitive Dynamics
- Doubling down on Bolt category (12% of order volumes) that Zomato recently abandoned
- Facing increased competitive pressure from both existing players and new entrants
- Retention improving with each cohort (4-6% higher than prior cohorts), indicating habit formation without ongoing discounts
- Shifting breakeven guidance to show flexibility rather than failure

- **Scale economics via density**: Higher GOV/orders per store improve infrastructure utilisation and unlock bigger brand-monetisation opportunities.
- **Warehousing build-out**: More than doubled warehousing capacity over the last four quarters (a key capex component); expects a little more warehousing capex to structurally improve supply-chain efficiency.
- **No "bad growth"**: Refusing to spend on customers retained only via heavy discounts — prioritising sustainable, profitable growth over vanity order volumes (e.g., 99-rupee orders).
- **MaxSaver working**: Larger-basket MaxSaver initiative lifts revenue per order; retention of MaxSaver customers is at least as high as non-MaxSaver.
- **Mature cohorts stickier**: Long-tenure users ordering more frequently; management wants flexibility in spend rather than chasing low-value orders.
- **Non-grocery mix stabilising**: Non-grocery (electronics, home) now >30% of quick commerce; monetisation initiatives will converge the ratio higher rather than mix expanding further.
- **Throughput target**: Aiming for a 25–30% increase in throughput per dark store via densification rather than adding many more stores.
- **Path to break-even**: ~30% of delivery polygons are currently positive; needs >50% positive polygons for overall break-even — to be achieved via added monetisation and contribution-margin improvement.

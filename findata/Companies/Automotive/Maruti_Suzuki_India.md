# Maruti Suzuki India

## Company Overview
Maruti Suzuki India Limited, incorporated in 1981 (formerly Maruti Udyog Limited, renamed September 2007) and headquartered in New Delhi, is India's largest passenger vehicle manufacturer. A subsidiary of Suzuki Motor Corporation, it manufactures and sells passenger vehicles, utility vehicles and multi-purpose vehicles, and facilitates pre-owned car sales, fleet management, car financing, driving schools, insurance and accessories. Maruti achieved a record 2.1 million units in FY24-25 (+6% YoY) and is executing a massive capacity expansion to double production to 4 million units by 2030 while transitioning its portfolio toward hybrids and electric vehicles.

## Key Insights (from Edition #23, Mar 2026)
- Doubling production to 4 million units by 2030 reflects a massive bet on India's structural demand growth, designed to secure a pre-emptive advantage over domestic and global rivals; FY24-25 already achieved a record 2.1 million units (+6% YoY).
- India's vehicle penetration is just 30 per 1,000 people (vs much higher in US/UK/Germany/Japan/China), implying a multi-decade structural growth runway as per-capita incomes rise; the country is approaching the $3,500 income inflection point that historically triggers a surge in car ownership (mirroring China's trajectory).
- The September 2025 GST revision acts as a catalyst for entry-level demand by narrowing the price gap with premium motorcycles — first-time buyer share jumped from 41% (Apr-Aug 2025) to 48% (Sep-Dec 2025); Maruti is running proactive 2W→4W upgrade promotions.
- Sales mix is shifting decisively from entry-level Mini toward Compacts and SUVs (SUV share ~55% of Indian PV market) — a favourable mix evolution supporting higher unit economics and margin expansion; 7 new SUVs planned in the next 5-6 years.
- EV strategy (e Vitara) directly addresses customer anxieties via Battery-as-a-Service (₹10.99 lakh + ₹3.99/km) and 60% assured buyback after 3 years — aggressive financial measures to secure early EV adopters while charging infrastructure matures.

**Margin hit from commodity + payment-cycle change:** Commodity costs took ~300 bps off margins this quarter, of which ~110 bps came from a temporary switch of the supplier settlement cycle (quarterly → monthly lag) for aluminium, plastics and rubber to ease supplier working-capital stress. Normalisation to the quarterly cycle over coming quarters flows the benefit back.

**Exports > 55% of India's PV exports:** Maruti alone exports more passenger vehicles than the other 16 Indian OEMs combined — a meaningful hedge against domestic cyclicality.

**Demand outstrips supply; 10% headroom:** Sales this year are constrained by the supply side, not demand; management sees ~10% growth headroom, gated entirely by how fast new capacity ramps.

**Aluminium off its peak:** Aluminium prices have started easing from highs — expected to lower costs and support H2 profitability.

**Gujarat EV hub — ₹3,900 cr fourth line:** The fourth line at Hansalpur (Gujarat) — predominantly an EV line — takes the plant into the global top-10 by scale; many more SUVs planned to fill whitespace in the lineup.

## The Chatter — Maruti Lumino Indian Bank

**Festive demand:** Onam retail in Kerala was very strong — van market share in Kerala reached 60%, a first in Maruti's history; management expects a further surge in sales through the festive season.

**Capacity ramp:** Two new assembly lines of 1 lakh units each are commissioned, ramping to full capacity over another 2–3 months. July produced ~2 lakh vehicles with nothing left at the factory — all dispatched to channel partners — and network stock stands at just 16 days.

**August dip was calendar, not demand:** Three fewer working days (Independence Day, Raksha Bandhan, an extra Sunday) at ~8,000 vehicles/day ≈ 24,000 units of domestic output — "absolutely no demand issue".

**Margins:** Maruti is absorbing a portion of commodity inflation (margin pressure) and will not run festive promotions, since promotions would mean raising prices first. It was the last OEM among peers to raise prices, but may pass on a portion of costs in the near future if input costs persist.

**Green mix:** Board-approved biogas bet — CBG blended with CNG gives a fuel profile effectively as clean as an EV in India; management believes EV penetration stays below 16–17% even by 2030, while CNG already accounts for 42% of total sales.

# Policy Debate

### Macro & Capital Markets | Policy Special | Taxation & Financial Architecture


A specialised macroeconomic dialogue featuring Uday Kotak, Founder and Non
Executive Director of Kotak Mahindra Bank, alongside Ananth Narayan

Gopalakrishnan, former Whole-Time Member of SEBI, examining the structural

distortions governing Indian capital flows. The discussion addresses the mechanics

behind foreign portfolio outflows, the case for OECD-aligned residence-based

taxation, and how the wide tax disparity between fixed income and public equities

suppresses domestic bank deposits while fueling artificial equity valuations.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lpQo3rJkX6Y)


Management identifies a major tax imbalance where interest income is taxed at nearly

triple the rate of equity gains. This creates a structural distortion that discourages

traditional savings in bank deposits and fixed income products.


_“There are two distinct points we must address. The first is establishing capital gains tax_

_parity between foreign institutional investors and Indian investors. If a distinction must be_

_drawn, foreign investors’ capital gains tax should be assessed on a dollar-currency basis_

_rather than a rupee basis, while Indian investors continue to be taxed in local currency._

_That framework preserves parity while respecting the functional currency of the respective_

_investor. The second point concerns domestic asset allocation. While there is continuous_


_clamour in India to lower the equity capital gains tax rate from 12.5% plus surcharge—_

_which totals roughly 14%—the taxation on interest income stands at the highest marginal_

_rate of 39%. The gap between a 14% tax on equities and 39% on fixed income is_

_exceptionally wide, and that disparity needs to be narrowed.”_


_— Uday Kotak, Founder & Non-Executive Director_


India’s practice of taxing foreign investors at the source makes the country an outlier

compared to global OECD standards. Moving to a residence-based tax model would

align India with major global markets and potentially reduce friction for foreign

capital.


_“The starting point is that the global norm for non-controlling portfolio equity investments_

_is to tax the investor in their country of residence, not at source. This is the established_

_OECD standard followed by virtually every major financial jurisdiction. When Indian_

_residents invest in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, or Singapore,_

_we do not pay capital gains tax in those jurisdictions. We declare our foreign earnings to_

_the Indian tax authority and pay tax domestically. India remains an outlier by insisting that_

_foreigners pay capital gains tax at source. This is not about exempting foreign capital while_

_taxing domestic investors; it is about respecting global residence-based taxation.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


Existing tax treaties fail to prevent double taxation for many large global funds that

are tax-exempt at home. This creates a literal cost to investing in India that cannot be

recovered through tax credits in the investor’s home country.


_“While the Finance Ministry often points to Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs)_

_to prevent double taxation, the mechanism breaks down in practice. First, many_

_institutional pools—such as sovereign wealth funds, public pension funds, and university_

_endowments—are tax-exempt in their home jurisdictions. When India levies a withholding_

_tax, these funds have zero domestic tax liability to offset it against, resulting in deadweight_

_cost. Second, while individual investors in the United States pay a 20% federal capital gains_

_rate, those investing via pooled vehicles face funds that pay withholding tax in India. The_

_fund provides a provision statement upon redemption, but US tax rules do not allow an_

_individual to claim a foreign tax credit against personal liabilities for a provision made by a_

_fund.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


Foreign investors often face tax liabilities in India even when their investments have

lost value in their local currency due to rupee depreciation. The heavy administrative

burden of getting tax clearances for every trade further complicates the repatriation of

capital.


_“Third, as Uday pointed out, India calculates capital gains on a rupee basis. Because the_

_rupee depreciates over time, a global investor can experience a loss in dollar, euro, or_

_sterling terms and still be forced to pay capital gains tax in India. Compounding this is the_

_operational nightmare for global custodians and asset managers like Vanguard, who must_

_engage local tax vendors to secure clearance certificates for every single transaction before_

_repatriating capital. Outside of narrow exceptions like physical real estate or permanent_

_establishments, India’s insistence on source-based withholding makes us an unnecessary_

_outlier at a time when we require our fair share of global savings.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


Recent years have seen a sharp reversal from massive inflows to significant outflows of

foreign capital, coinciding with higher capital gains taxes. Global fund managers

continue to cite India’s tax structure as a primary reason for their caution despite the

country’s growth potential.


_“While correlation is not causation, the empirical record tells a sobering story. In FY19,_

_India reintroduced the 10% long-term capital gains tax on equities. From FY19 to date,_

_cumulative net FPI equity flows have turned into a net outflow of roughly $10 billion. By_

_comparison, the previous decade from FY09 to FY18 saw net inflows of $100 billion, and_

_the decade prior recorded positive inflows of $65 billion. Following the subsequent hike in_

_the LTCG rate to 12.5%, net foreign portfolio outflows between FY25 and FY27 have_

_approached $50 billion. Naturally, this is a multi-variable equation influenced by global_

_interest rates, geopolitical tensions across Ukraine and the Middle East, and the artificial_

_intelligence boom in the United States. However, in conversations with institutional_

_allocators across Tokyo, Singapore, and Paris, India’s source-based taxation remains an_

_acute structural irritant.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


The Indian debt market has been artificially suppressed by central bank interventions

and high taxes, preventing it from functioning as a free market. This environment has

historically flooded banks with cheap liquidity but penalised the savers who provide it.


_“It is less about preferential treatment for equities and more about the stepchild treatment_

_of fixed income. The debt market suffers from a combination of financial repression and_

_punitive taxation. Over recent years, the Reserve Bank of India purchased ₹11.6 lakh crore_

_of government securities—roughly 2.5% of GDP annually across FY25 and FY26—to_

_suppress sovereign yields and support growth. While the central bank is now net selling_

_bonds, it previously facilitated $143 billion of FCNR(B) deposit inflows and swapped them_

_at subsidised rates, flooding commercial banks with cheap liquidity. As a result, interest_

_rates have been managed by policy interventions rather than determined by the equilibrium_

_of willing savers and borrowers.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


High earners in India currently face negative real returns on bank deposits after

paying taxes, causing them to move money into stocks and gold. This flight of capital

explains the current difficulty banks are having in growing their deposit bases.


_“When artificial rate suppression is compounded by charging the highest marginal tax rate_

_of 39% on interest income, post-tax returns fail to beat real inflation perceptions. A 7%_

_fixed deposit yield yields negative real returns for anyone in the 30% to 39% bracket. Savers_

_logically abandon fixed income and redirect capital into assets offering inflation protection,_

_specifically equities, gold, and real estate. This explains why commercial banks face an_

_uphill battle mobilising deposits.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


India’s financial system is heavily skewed toward equity, with a credit market that is

significantly smaller relative to its stock market than global averages. This imbalance

suggests the domestic lending and bond markets are severely underdeveloped for an

economy of India’s size.


_“The consequences for capital formation are profound. Non-government credit—spanning_

_bank loans, NBFC lending, and corporate bonds—stands at just 65% of equity market_

_capitalisation in India. That is the lowest credit-to-equity ratio globally, compared to 95%_

_in the United States, between 125% and 195% across mature economies, and a global norm_

_of 140%. We have cultivated a buoyant equity market while structurally stunting our credit_

_and debt markets.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


Domestic tax policy is unintentionally pumping the stock market full of retail cash,

driving up valuations to levels that cause foreign investors to sell. Correcting this

requires either higher interest rates or lower taxes on savings to balance out where

money flows.


_“Because tax policy penalises debt, an enormous flood of domestic retail liquidity is forced_

_directly into equities, sustaining elevated valuation multiples across the market. These rich_

_valuations make it compelling for foreign institutional investors to take profits and_

_withdraw capital, compounding foreign outflows. To resolve this distortion, policymakers_

_must either allow market interest rates to rise to levels that compensate domestic savers_

_post-tax, or lower the tax burden on interest income.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_


Simplifying the tax on interest to a flat 20% could stabilise the financial system by

drawing excess liquidity out of the stock market. This move would likely lower equity

valuations to more sustainable levels and help build a stronger corporate bond market.


_“If taxation on interest income were rationalised to a flat 20%, a meaningful portion of_

_domestic capital would migrate out of overheated equity segments back into bank deposits_

_and corporate bonds. That rebalancing would normalise equity multiples, revitalise the_

_domestic credit market, and create a balanced asset framework capable of attracting_

_institutional foreign capital across both debt and equity.”_


_— Ananth Narayan Gopalakrishnan, Former Whole-Time Member, SEBI_

# Automobiles

### Mahindra & Mahindra Limited | Large Cap


Mahindra & Mahindra is a diversified Indian conglomerate and a global leader in

tractor manufacturing and the Indian SUV market. The company operates across

multiple sectors including financial services, technology, and hospitality through its

various subsidiaries.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uOsm0u3b4Cw)


Management successfully protected profits during the quarter by using price hikes and

cost-cutting measures to fight inflation. Investors should see this as a sign of high

operational efficiency and the ability to maintain strong returns on equity even in a

tough market.


_“The defining keyword for the second quarter was resilience. Performance came in_

_significantly ahead of market expectations despite navigating substantial operational_

_challenges throughout the period. Geopolitical tensions drove commodity inflation across_

_multiple inputs, requiring proactive interventions to prevent cost pressures from diluting our_

_bottom line and key return metrics. Our operating teams executed an all-hands-on-deck_

_effort across disciplined pricing actions, structural cost takeout programs, and operating_

_leverage. That operational rigour delivered 25% revenue growth alongside a 21% return on_

_equity. More importantly, this was a synchronised performance where every single business_

_contributed to group earnings.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_


The company’s diverse subsidiaries, particularly in finance and technology, are

showing strong profit growth simultaneously. This trend suggests that the company’s

value is being driven by multiple engines rather than just its core automotive business.


_“We are seeing the true compounding power of the Mahindra federation. Our Growth Gems_

_tripled their profit contribution, Mahindra Finance surged 61% with a meaningful earnings_

_impact, and Tech Mahindra continues on a strong turnaround trajectory, up 33%. Across_

_India, the underlying economic resilience has been remarkable. Despite crude volatility,_

_input inflation, and higher borrowing costs, consumption remains exceptionally robust.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_


Vehicle demand is so high that the company is struggling to expand production

quickly enough to keep up. This massive order backlog provides a clear and visible

revenue runway for the upcoming quarters.


_“In automotive, we are seeing 20% volume growth, and we simply cannot build vehicles fast_

_enough. Even after quadrupling our capacity and preparing to add another tranche in the_

_coming months, demand continues to outstrip supply. Looking into the second half,_

_inflationary pressures are stabilising, supported by softer crude prices.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_


The company is shutting down loss-making international businesses to improve the

overall profitability of its farm division. This strategic cleanup should result in higher

margins for the segment regardless of local sales volume.


_“Internationally, where we faced market headwinds, we executed decisive portfolio_

_cleanups by shutting down operations across three underperforming entities. That_

_rationalisation will substantially enhance international farm profitability. Consequently, on_

_a consolidated basis across domestic and global markets, our farm equipment business will_

_emerge stronger and more profitable than last year.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_


The company is aggressively increasing its manufacturing space and expects these

new plants to be fully utilised the moment they open. This rapid expansion is

necessary to clear existing customer orders and convert them into revenue.


_“We are expanding automotive manufacturing capacity by 27% this fiscal year and expect_

_that expanded capacity to operate at near 100% utilisation almost immediately upon_

_commissioning. Next year, we will add another 10,000 units of monthly capacity, which we_

_also anticipate will run at full utilisation. Our primary operational challenge right now is_

_adding capacity fast enough to fulfil outstanding order banks.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_


A massive new plant in Nagpur is intended to double the company’s manufacturing

capacity within two years. Management plans to maintain some spare capacity to

ensure they can always meet surges in customer demand without long waiting lists.


_“Looking further out, our greenfield Nagpur plant is designed to double our manufacturing_

_footprint over the next two years. Ultimately, as an operating philosophy, I would rather_

_run facilities at 85% to 90% capacity utilisation with a built-in buffer than operate at 100%_

_and leave customer demand unfulfilled on the table.”_


_— Dr. Anish Shah, Managing Director and CEO_

# Financial Services


### Life Insurance Corporation of India | Large Cap

Life Insurance Corporation of India is the country’s largest life insurer, maintaining a

dominant market share through its vast agency force and growing digital presence.

The company offers a comprehensive range of individual and group insurance

solutions, including participating, non-participating, and unit-linked products.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VG8WbzpoQ3k)


Management is focusing on selling more protection policies, which are more

profitable but show up differently in growth metrics than traditional savings plans.

They expect the gap between total premium growth and these metrics to close as

market conditions improve later this year.


_“Our primary strategic focus over the past quarter has been on strengthening our individual_

_and group protection portfolio. Protection products inherently carry different premium_

_payment structures compared to large single-premium savings contracts, meaning the_

_volume expansion in protection does not translate into an immediate, outsized spike in_

_reported APE. Concurrently, heightened equity market volatility led to subdued consumer_

_and distributor appetite for market-linked products like ULIPs, which further restrained_

_APE expansion during the quarter. However, as operating conditions stabilise, we are_

_confident that this divergence will narrow significantly over the second half of the fiscal_

_year.”_


_— R. Doraiswamy, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer_


The company expects sales of market-linked products to bounce back following recent

stock market volatility and distributor caution. Management is signalling a stronger

performance for the second half of the year driven by their existing distribution

network and a better product mix.


_“As an institutional policy, LIC does not issue forward-looking quantitative guidance_

_targets. What we have consistently maintained is an unwavering focus on profitable,_

_sustainable growth, and our operating performance reflects that commitment. To address_

_the first part of your question, the correction in domestic equity indices and broader market_

_volatility during the second quarter dampened distributor momentum and customer_

_demand for ULIPs. With market sentiment improving and our simultaneous push across_

_high-margin protection and participating savings products, we expect customer interest in_

_ULIPs to rebound in the coming months. Supported by this product mix, we anticipate_


_strong momentum in the second half that will drive healthy, full-year APE growth. While I_

_will not commit to a specific percentage, our underlying distribution strength positions us_

_for an accelerated performance.”_


_— R. Doraiswamy, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer_


The CEO clarifies that recent margin improvements are primarily due to better

internal operations rather than just changes in interest rates. This is important for

investors as it suggests that profitability gains are sustainable and not just a result of

temporary market movements.


_“That perception is inaccurate. Positive operational variance has been a substantial and_

_structural contributor to our expanding VNB margins over successive periods, driven by_

_ongoing internal operational efficiencies. Second, while ULIPs witnessed a contraction in_

_conventional APE terms, they generated positive growth on a Weighted Received Premium_

_basis. LIC markets both single-premium and regular-premium ULIPs, and we saw healthy_

_traction across single-premium structures, ensuring our aggregate inflows remained_

_resilient. There was a modest contribution to reported margins from risk-free rate shifts and_

_yield-curve movements, but we do not disclose interim line-item reconciliations; the_

_complete VNB walk will be presented at the close of the financial year. Our core focus_

_remains on enhancing operational cost discipline and driving profitable underwriting.”_


_— R. Doraiswamy, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer_


LIC is diversifying how it sells policies to rely less on individual agents, with bank

partnerships now making up nearly 8% of new business. While the sale of IDBI Bank

might change their exclusive deal, management believes their long-term relationship

will protect their sales volumes.


_“Over the past three years, LIC has systematically broadened its distribution architecture,_

_expanding the contribution of bancassurance, corporate agents, brokers, and alternate_

_channels to roughly 7.8% to 8% of total new business premium. On the banking side, IDBI_

_Bank remains one of our premier partners and holds an exclusive pan-India bancassurance_

_tie-up that contributes meaningfully to our volumes. As the government’s divestment_

_process advances, a strategic promoter will acquire controlling equity, and if that group_

_houses an existing life insurance arm, our exclusive arrangement may evolve. However,_

_given our operational depth and entrenched relationship across IDBI Bank’s branch_


_network, we do not foresee any decline in the overall volume of business originated through_

_the partnership.”_


_— R. Doraiswamy, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer_


LIC must reduce its ownership in IDBI Bank to 15% to follow standard banking rules

once the government completes its sale of the bank. This provides a clear two-year

timeline for LIC to sell its remaining excess shares and bring its capital allocation in

line with regulations.


_“Current Reserve Bank of India banking regulations strictly prohibit an insurance company_

_from holding a majority controlling stake in a commercial bank. LIC was granted an_

_extraordinary regulatory dispensation by the authorities at the time, given the capital_

_requirements and financial stress prevailing at IDBI Bank. Once the government divestment_

_concludes and majority control transfers to the incoming promoter, standard regulatory_

_frameworks will be reinstated. Under banking regulations, LIC will be required to bring its_

_residual shareholding down to the statutory threshold of 15%. Upon closing the current_

_transaction, LIC’s stake will reduce to approximately 19%, and we will utilise the permitted_

_two-year regulatory window to orderly pare the remaining equity down to the mandated_

_15% level.”_


_— R. Doraiswamy, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer_

# Engineering & Capital Goods

### Bharat Forge Limited | Large Cap


Bharat Forge is a global technology-driven metal forming company specialising in

safety-critical components for the automotive, energy, and defence sectors. The firm is

currently undergoing a strategic pivot toward high-margin domestic manufacturing

and advanced technology applications.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wiyjIICzrak)


Management is navigating short-term profitability pressure caused by higher input

costs and delayed pricing adjustments. Investors should note the ambitious target to

reach 28% margins by next year as these cost pressures ease.


_“Regarding margins, performance was definitely impacted by raw material inflation_

_alongside the customary pass-through lag. We expect margins to improve quarter-on-_

_quarter and recover to significantly higher levels than where we stand today. Our_

_operational objective is to return toward the 28% mark by next fiscal year.”_


_— Amit Kalyani, Vice Chairman and Joint Managing Director_


The company is undergoing significant structural changes in Europe while

simultaneously investing heavily in new domestic capacity. The massive 1,350 crore

capital expenditure program is expected to drive significant revenue growth starting in

early 2025.


_“We are absorbing certain exceptional costs tied to the restructuring of our German entity,_

_which remains on track and will be fully completed by the middle of next year. On the top_

_line, export growth has been robust across both automotive and non-automotive sectors. On_

_the industrial front, we have secured approximately 2,000 crore of new business over the_

_past six months. These orders will begin ramping up early next year as our newly added_

_capacities come online. This represents one of our largest capex programs at close to_

_1,350 crore, starting commercial commissioning sequentially from the January–March_

_quarter of this year through the end of next year.”_


_— Amit Kalyani, Vice Chairman and Joint Managing Director_


Defence margins were temporarily suppressed because the company hired staff and

built infrastructure before production actually started. Now that the ATAGS system

has received bulk production clearance, investors can expect revenue and margin

recovery in the defence segment starting next fiscal year.


_“The margin contraction was driven by one-time development expenses for new products_

_that have secured healthy order pipelines, combined with preparatory overheads. We had_

_prepared manpower and operational infrastructure in anticipation of an earlier ramp-up_

_for the Advanced Towed Artillery Gun System (ATAGS), so we absorbed those advance_

_costs ahead of volume delivery. The positive development is that we received Bulk_

_Production Clearance (BPC) for ATAGS roughly two weeks ago. Deliveries will commence_

_from the first quarter of next fiscal year, allowing us to ship whatever volume the armed_

_forces require.”_


_— Amit Kalyani, Vice Chairman and Joint Managing Director_


Bharat Forge is aggressively shifting its manufacturing weight back to India, aiming

for 90% of revenue to come from domestic plants. This strategy reduces reliance on

struggling overseas units and focuses resources on the more profitable Indian

operations.


_“Currently, domestic manufacturing accounts for approximately 65% to 70% of total_

_revenue, with overseas operations contributing 30% to 35%. Within two to three years, we_

_expect India-based manufacturing revenue to expand to roughly 90% of our consolidated_

_business. By FY29, domestic operations—including standalone Bharat Forge and domestic_

_subsidiaries like defence, Kalyani Powertrain, and JS Auto Cast—will account for 80% to_

_85% of total revenue. Simultaneously, our objective is to bring overseas subsidiaries to_

_operational break-even so they eliminate losses and function as strategic beachheads to_

_capture global contracts.”_


_— Amit Kalyani, Vice Chairman and Joint Managing Director_


The company is in a very strong liquidity position with substantial cash on hand and

incoming advances from defence contracts. This financial cushion allows them to

execute large projects without needing more equity capital or debt.


_“We recently concluded a 2,000 crore fundraise and currently hold 4,000 crore in cash,_

_so there is no requirement for further fundraising. In addition, with the commencement of_

_ATAGS bulk production, we will receive working capital advances from the armed forces to_

_fund production. We also anticipate substantial defence contract awards following Defence_

_Acquisition Council (DAC) clearances for tenders where we have already been selected as_

_the preferred bidder, which will be significantly larger than orders secured in the first half.”_


_— Amit Kalyani, Vice Chairman and Joint Managing Director_

# Software Services

### Mphasis Limited | Mid Cap


Mphasis is a global information technology solutions provider specialising in cloud

and cognitive services. The company focuses on architecture-led transformation to

help enterprise clients modernise legacy systems and integrate artificial intelligence

into core operations.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kW8PuwWIuQQ)


While traditional discretionary IT spending remains muted, companies are pivoting

toward short-cycle, high-impact investments in corporate integration and market

expansion. This shift benefits specialised providers who can guarantee business

outcomes rather than just providing general technical labour.


_“I would not say those concerns were entirely unfounded, because traditional discretionary_

_spending as the industry historically defined it has not returned. Instead, current enterprise_

_spending is strictly directed toward mission-critical foundational investments. That has_

_generated a concentrated burst of shorter-cycle spending across corporate integrations,_

_market expansions, and targeted revenue-unlocking initiatives, which has expanded our_

_addressable market. However, this recovery will not lift all IT providers equally. Realising_

_these gains requires acute specialisation across technical architecture, vertical industry_

_domain knowledge, deep enterprise context, and the ability to guarantee operational_

_outcomes.”_


_— Nitin Rakesh, Chief Executive Officer_


The IT services industry is moving away from billing for hours worked toward long
term partnerships focused on implementing complete AI technology stacks. This

structural change in how clients buy technology services is forcing a modernisation of

service delivery frameworks.


_“The industry’s commercial engagement model is fundamentally shifting away from_

_traditional time-and-materials, input-led efficiency deals toward multi-year transformation_

_partnerships where enterprises progressively deploy the full AI stack at scale. Enterprise_

_consumption patterns are undergoing a profound evolution. Chief Information Officers are_

_explicitly directing their technology leadership teams to abandon legacy ways of working in_

_favour of modern frameworks, fundamentally altering both the enterprise buying centre and_

_the strategic outcomes clients expect technology partners to deliver.”_


_— Nitin Rakesh, Chief Executive Officer_


The company has narrowed its full-year revenue growth guidance to the low double
digit range following strong deal intake in the first half. This reflects high

management confidence and visibility in client spending despite broader

macroeconomic fluctuations.


_“We began the fiscal year guiding for high-single-digit to low-double-digit revenue growth._

_Backed by our performance across the first two quarters, our expanding pipeline, and recent_

_large deal closures, we have firmed up our expectations toward the low double-digit mark._

_Fortunately, underlying corporate IT budgets and strategic enterprise initiatives do not_

_fluctuate with the daily news cycle or market sentiment. Enterprise clients formulate_

_deliberate, long-term operational plans and execute them systematically. Supported by our_

_sustained deal intake and the operational drivers Nitin outlined, we have strong visibility_

_and confidence in meeting our stated full-year guidance.”_


_— Aravind Viswanathan, Chief Financial Officer_


The new Tria AI platform is moving into live production with clients and facilitating a

shift toward outcome-based revenue-sharing models. This evolution suggests a

potential improvement in earnings quality by aligning company compensation with

client revenue growth.


_“It is slightly premature to publish isolated financial metrics regarding specific client counts_

# Defence

### BEML Limited | Small Cap


BEML is a public sector undertaking that manufactures a wide range of heavy

equipment for mining, construction, defence, and rail and metro segments. It is a key

player in India’s defence and urban infrastructure sectors, supplying specialised

vehicles and rolling stock.


[[Interview]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AMKH-d3eFHo)


Management explains that current low margins are due to high raw material costs and

a focus on lower-margin metro projects in the first half. Investors should watch for a

significant margin recovery in the second half as the company shifts production to

higher-margin mining and defence equipment.


_“Top-line momentum has been exceptionally strong through the first half, and we expect_

_that pace to sustain across the third and fourth quarters, allowing us to maintain robust_

_full-year growth. On operating margins, EBITDA currently stands at 4.4%, primarily_

_because capital goods manufacturing involves long lead cycles where revenue conversion_

_and higher profitability are heavily back-ended into the second half. Margin performance is_

_fundamentally dictated by raw material costs as a percentage of the value of production,_

_which rose year-on-year during the first half. In our portfolio, material cost intensity varies_

_significantly by division: raw material costs represent roughly 48% of value in mining_

_equipment, 52% to 53% in commuter rail, 58% to 60% in defence, and approximately 80%_

_in metro rolling stock. As execution shifts in the third and fourth quarters toward high-_

_margin mining equipment, commuter rail, and export contracts, profitability will expand_

_substantially. I remain confident we will meet our original double-digit EBITDA margin_

_guidance and return to normalised FY25 levels.”_


_— Shantanu Roy, Chairman & Managing Director_


The company has officially increased its full-year revenue growth target from 30% to

# Financial Services

### Bajaj Finserv Limited | Large Cap


Bajaj Finserv is a diversified financial services conglomerate with major operations in

lending, asset management, and insurance. It serves as the holding company for its key

subsidiaries, including Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Allianz insurance companies.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/5167-03-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


Management is addressing the potential regulatory changes regarding insurance

distribution economics that could disrupt current fee structures. They believe that

while initial implementation might cause short-term volatility, the industry

structurally improves after such reforms.


_“However, as has been seen in the past, any large-scale initiative that aims to improve the_

_customer proposition may lead to a short-term impact for insurers as well as distributors in_

_this case. However, in the medium to long term, the sector only bounces back much stronger,_

_as has been seen in the past in terms of GST exemptions, various changes in ULIP norms,_

_or surrender value changes, and so on.”_


_— Ramandeep Singh Sahni, CFO, Bajaj Finserv Limited_


The headline growth for the general insurance segment was weighed down by the

company’s decision to avoid certain low-margin government tenders. The core retail

business in health and motor is actually growing much faster than the overall market

rate.


_“GWP for Bajaj General grew 10% for the quarter to about 7,556 crore, which is marginally_

_lower than the industry growth of about 12%. However, if we were to exclude the bulky_

_tender-driven crop and government health business, GWP for the company increased by a_

_very healthy 21%, which is significantly higher than the industry growth of close to 13%._

_This was primarily driven by the health and motor segments, which grew 31% and 18%,_

_respectively.”_


_— Ramandeep Singh Sahni, CFO, Bajaj Finserv Limited_


The life insurance division has successfully transitioned toward more profitable

product categories, leading to significant margin expansion. This suggests the

company is effectively balancing volume growth with high-quality earnings.


_“VNB for the quarter grew 40% to 512 crore, and NBM was up at 21.6% for the quarter, as_

_against 17.1% for the same quarter last year, an expansion of 4.5%. I am also happy to_

_inform you that on a rolling 12-month basis, despite the impact of GST, our life insurance_

_business has crossed an NBM of 20% and ended at 20.8%.”_


_— Ramandeep Singh Sahni, CFO, Bajaj Finserv Limited_


Management has deliberately reduced exposure to non-participating products due to

high pricing competition, favouring the annuity segment instead. This tactical shift


has allowed the company to maintain a stable product mix and avoid low-margin

business.


_“We thought it was better to move to another space, and we found an opportunity in_

_annuity. That was when we launched different products and pursued some new_

_opportunities in annuity. That has held up since then. If you look at Q1 last year, our non-_

_par was about 18-odd per cent and annuity was 5%, and in 2 quarters the mix changed to_

_about 13% non-par and annuity at about 10%, plus or minus. I think that base has been_

_holding up, so for almost a year we have seen our non-par and annuity running in this_

_profile.”_


_— S. Sreenivasan, President, Insurance and Special Projects_


While the company delivered a massive increase in margins this quarter, leadership is

warning investors not to project this level of growth indefinitely. They are setting a

more conservative expectation for margin sustainability going forward.


_“The only thing is that we would not like to raise expectations regarding the expansion. The_

_expansion has been very high in this quarter as well, which I believe not many people were_

_expecting in our last call. The 4.6% expansion is very good, but I do not see this kind of_

_expansion being sustained. So I would lower your expectations on that.”_


_— Tarun Chugh, MD and CEO, Bajaj Life Insurance Limited_


Bajaj Finserv has a strong cash position of 4,700 crore and expects further capital

release from new accounting standards. This liquidity is dedicated to funding new

initiatives like the asset management business and reinsurance venture without

needing external debt.


_“Currently, we are sitting on a surplus of about 5,000 crores of capital, to be precise, about_

_4,700 crores. As you know, we have been receiving dividends from all the material_

_subsidiaries, whether it is BFL or the insurance companies. That 4,700 crores plus the_

_dividends should be sufficient to fund these investments going forward. We would be happy_

_to have more surplus, and hence we will also have to wait and watch what IRDAI decides in_

_terms of the surplus that will be generated upon the one-time migration to Ind AS.”_


_— Ramandeep Singh Sahni, CFO, Bajaj Finserv Limited_


# Healthcare

### Zydus Lifesciences | Large Cap

Zydus Lifesciences is a leading global pharmaceutical company headquartered in India

that discovers, develops, and manufactures a wide range of healthcare therapies. The

company maintains a strong presence in the generic, speciality, and innovative

pharmaceutical markets across India, the United States, and emerging economies.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/4331-04-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


The company has achieved successful clinical trial results for Saroglitazar in two

major liver disease indications, PBC and MASH. These results provide critical clinical

validation and data to support regulatory approvals and market entry in the United

States.


_“On the NCE front, we presented new analysis for the Phase 3 clinical trial of Saroglitazar_

_Magnesium in the PBC indication at the American College of Gastroenterology, or ACG,_

_2026 annual meeting. The analysis demonstrated consistent biochemical response benefits_

_versus placebo across geographic subgroups, including both US and non-US patients. The_

_findings build on the clinical profile of Saroglitazar in patients with PBC who have an_

_inadequate response or intolerance to UDCA. We successfully completed the Evidence-IV_

_global Phase 2b trial evaluating Saroglitazar Magnesium in the MASH indication. The_

_study successfully met its primary endpoint, demonstrating resolution of steatohepatitis_

_with no worsening of fibrosis at 52 weeks. We also observed encouraging improvements_

_across several key secondary and exploratory efficacy measures.”_


_— Dr. Sharvil Patel, Managing Director_


The US business is rapidly moving away from standard generics toward high-value

speciality products and biosimilars. The significant sequential jump in speciality

revenue share shows that the company’s recent acquisitions and internal pipeline are

beginning to scale.


_“Our differentiated 505(b)(2) products, rare disease offerings, and biologics pipeline are_

_creating powerful new growth vectors. Rolvedon continues to gain momentum, while the_

_USFDA’s acceptance of the BLA for the Pembrolizumab biosimilar... represents a major_

_milestone in our biologics journey. Alongside the upcoming launch of Saroglitazar, these_


_catalysts position us for the next phase of growth. Notably, specialty products accounted for_

_16% of US revenues during the quarter, up approximately 550 basis points sequentially,_

_underscoring the pace and success of our strategic transformation.”_


_— Dr. Sharvil Patel, Managing Director_


Zydus plans to launch its key asset, Saroglitazar, in the US market by March, incurring

annual commercialisation costs of roughly $100 million. Investors should note the

deliberate timing of the launch to avoid the year-end holiday period while managing a

significant new cost base.


_“We are looking for a March launch, and obviously we would still see hiring of the sales_

_field force and other front-end teams continuing. So we expect our commercial expense to_

_be around 100 million dollars every year. We are currently on track with our responses._

_November is our current GDUFA date and, as I said, our current expectation is to launch_

_in March. Because obviously we are not launching in December. It is the year-end, and it_

_does not make sense to launch at the end of the year.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_


The company expects to consistently outperform the Indian Pharmaceutical Market

growth rate by 2% to 4% annually. This sustained outperformance in the domestic

market provides a stable, high-growth foundation for the overall business.


_“The last 2 quarters have seen very strong performance versus the IPM. The IPM, I think,_

_obviously had some base issues because of GST and other things. We still believe that the_

_market will grow between 10% and 11%, and we will definitely grow 200 to 400 basis points_

_consistently more than the IPM from a future point of view. Obviously, currently our growth_

_trajectory looks faster, and I think we will see that momentum continue.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_


Zydus faces a significant supply shortage in its transdermal patch business where

demand exceeds current capacity by 50%. The company plans to double its production

capacity within a year, which could unlock substantial revenue growth in this

specialised segment.


_“Right now, we are at capacity. We are selling as much as we can make. Demand in the_

_market is 50% more than what is currently being served, so there is significant unmet_

_demand. Obviously, no companies have the capacity to cater to it immediately. Building_


_new capacity also takes time in transdermals. Currently, the market has more demand than_

_actual availability. That is where we are. This business has also scaled up meaningfully for_

_us, and we are doing well. With additional capacity potentially coming in 1 year, we could_

_see it scale up further. It will take at least 1 year, but we could double our capacity.”_


_— Dr. Sharvil Patel, Managing Director_


Management is focusing R&D spending almost exclusively on complex and innovative

products rather than standard generics. This heavy investment, set at over 8% of sales,

is intended to build long-term competitive moats and protect future margins.


_“Conventional generic programs account for only about 10 to 12 of the 30 products we file._

_Most of the products we develop are either first-to-file products, complex generics,_

_biologics, vaccines, specialties, or NCEs. A large part of our development effort, and the_

_growth in R&D, is coming from these areas. As a percentage, we have guided that R&D_

_costs will be around 8% to 8.5%. We do not see R&D coming down for us, and it will_

_continue to grow because we are investing heavily. The future of the company also depends_

_on ensuring that we continue to have a differentiated specialty portfolio in both India and_

_the US.”_


_— Dr. Sharvil Patel, Managing Director_


While the company is using cash flow to reduce debt, it remains willing to maintain a

leverage ratio of up to 1x EBITDA to fund growth and capital expenditures. This

indicates a balanced approach between balance sheet health and aggressive

investment in manufacturing and R&D.


_“Whatever cash we generate, we will use it to pay down our debt, and it has come down_

_versus the last quarter. Net debt has come down from 5,900 crores to 5,200 crores. That will_

_obviously continue if we continue to generate cash. However, as I said, there is also an_

_uptick in the capex cycle as well as in all the investments. We are building for future growth,_

_and I think that is very critical for us over the next 5 years. We have always said that we are_

_comfortable with net debt-to-EBITDA of up to 1 time, and that is what we are guiding.”_


_— Dr. Sharvil Patel, Managing Director_

# Defence


### Bharat Electronics Ltd. | Large Cap

Bharat Electronics Limited is a premier Indian state-owned company that designs and

manufactures advanced electronic products for the defence and aerospace sectors. Its

portfolio includes complex systems such as radars, missile electronics, communication

equipment, and electronic warfare suites for the Indian Armed Forces.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/38-03-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


Management is nearly certain they will secure the massive QRSAM order due to their

decade-long development experience with DRDO. While private competition may

emerge in the future, the company’s entrenched position makes it the primary

beneficiary of complex system orders today.


_“As of now, I am more than 99% confident that this program is coming to us very soon. In_

_the future, if the government decides and the private sector reaches that level, there will_

_definitely be chances of private companies receiving different types of orders. They may_

_start with some missiles, as stated in some public statements, and later migrate to complex_

_systems as well. If the private sector comes up, it will be a real win-win situation over a_

_period of time.”_


_— Manoj Jain, Chairman & Managing Director_


The company anticipates receiving roughly 20,000 crores in orders for the Next

Generation Corvette program before the end of the fiscal year. This expected influx

represents a significant portion of their non-QRSAM order guidance for the year.


_“We are expecting orders worth around Rs.20,000 crore in a staggered manner, but 99% of_

_them will come in this financial year. As of now, it appears that there will be no slippage of_

_one or two line items. However, we had also hoped to receive the QRSAM order by March_

_last year, but there were some slippages because of procedural terms and conditions,_

_discussions, and so on. Therefore, one or two line items may slip, but as of today, it appears_

_that we may receive orders for all the NGC line items before March.”_


_— Manoj Jain, Chairman & Managing Director_


Management clarified that direct government nominations do not carry premium

margins compared to competitive bids. Instead, these nominations are earned by


winning initial competitions and proving the uniqueness of their home-grown

technology.


_“Whether it is nomination or competition, the margins will be more or less similar. In_

_nomination, we do not receive higher margins. The way we win, or the reason we win, is_

_based on the uniqueness of our product and the indigenization content developed over a_

_period of time in our technology. That is what differentiates us from others. Let me tell you_

_again that whatever nomination you see today actually began with multi-vendor_

_competition. We became L1, and only when the product proved suitable for the next ship or_

_the next order did they move to BNE, buyer nomination, or nomination.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_


Focusing on missiles is a high-margin strategy because electronic systems, where the

company excels, represent the majority of a missile’s value. This allows the firm to

capture the most lucrative portion of the growing domestic missile market.


_“We are definitely making missiles one of our focused areas of development because, as you_

_may know, electronics accounts for 60% to 70% of a missile. Sixty to 70% of the cost of a_

_missile goes into missile electronics. The remaining cost goes into 2 other major_

_components. Therefore, the biggest component of missiles is electronics, and we have now_

_demonstrated our strength in this area.”_


_— Management, Business Segment_


Management has built significant manufacturing capacity for missile seekers, which

are critical high-value components. The ability to quickly triple production without

major new infrastructure spending provides a competitive advantage as domestic

missile demand rises.


_“We can easily manufacture more than 1,000 combined IR and RF seekers at present. If the_

_need arises, we can scale this up by another 2-3 times. As you may know, seekers are similar_

_to radars. An RF seeker is similar to a radar, and an IR seeker is similar to an IR camera._

_Technologically, they are more or less similar. We have a good technological understanding_

_of this subject. Because we have large infrastructure for radars and IR-based sensors, it can_

_be converted very quickly to meet seeker requirements.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_


The company is successfully diversifying into civilian cybersecurity, which already has

a 500 crore order book and a pipeline worth thousands of crores. This move into non
defence critical infrastructure represents a major new avenue for high-tech revenue

growth.


_“That is where we are focusing as an additional business opportunity. That is why we_

_created a new SBU, Network and Cyber Security, as a separate SBU at our Bangalore_

_complex around 2 years ago, or 2.5 years ago. It has started delivering very good results in_

_the civilian domain as well, particularly in cybersecurity-related areas. We currently have_

_good orders in execution. I think we are executing orders worth approximately Rs.400-500_

_crore in the civilian cybersecurity domain. There are also opportunities and strong leads in_

_the pipeline that could convert into business worth thousands of crores.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_


Management reaffirmed their full-year guidance for 15% revenue growth and 28%

EBITDA margins despite the strong start to the year. The massive order inflow target

of up to 50,000 crores remains the key catalyst for the stock in the second half.


_“We are going to meet the guidance given at the start of the year. We are working toward_

_that. We are targeting revenue growth of more than 15%, although there may be some_

_positive surprises, as you have indicated. However, at present, we are confident that we will_

_achieve growth of more than 15%. We are targeting EBITDA margins of around 28%. We_

_are targeting order inflow of a minimum of Rs.25,000 crore excluding QRSAM and more_

_than Rs.50,000 crore including QRSAM. We are targeting R&D investment of more than_

_Rs.2,200 crore and capex of around Rs.1,200 crore.”_


_— Management, Executive Leadership_

# FMCG

### Marico Ltd. | Mid Cap


Marico is a leading Indian consumer goods company specialising in health, beauty,

and wellness products across domestic and international markets. The company

maintains a dominant position in the coconut oil and premium edible oil segments

while rapidly expanding its portfolio into digital-first brands and healthy foods.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/3891-02-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


Management has successfully upgraded its distribution network to increase direct

reach and rural presence. This structural change is now contributing consistent

incremental volume growth while reducing wasteful spending in the trade pipeline.


_“Pivotal to this strong performance is our GTM transformation under Project SETU, which_

_has given us a significant advantage in terms of better reach, assortment, rural distribution,_

_and sharper execution. Our direct reach has gone up by more than 1.5x, while we have_

_deepened our rural distribution by nearly doubling the number of stockists. We estimate_

_that with SETU, we have been able to drive 1% to 2% incremental volume growth in the_

_business over the last couple of years, with VAHO being the key portfolio benefiting from_

_this, and now PCNO receiving some benefits. In addition, SETU has delivered a far better_

_rhythm of business every day of the month, with significant savings in below-the-line spends_

_and a very thin stock pipeline.”_


_— Saugata Gupta, MD & CEO_


The hair oil business is shifting toward premium products that offer better profits and

faster growth. Management intends to turn the Almond Hair Oil brand into a major

market challenger with a significant revenue target by 2030.


_“Value-Added Hair Oils delivered its sixth consecutive quarter of stellar growth, with 21%_

_value growth and an 80 basis point gain in value market share on a MAT basis. This_

_consistency points to a structural shift in the portfolio’s growth strategy. It is driven by our_

_strategic pivot towards the mid and premium segments. This segment is both growth- and_

_margin-accretive for us, and delivered mid-teens volume growth in this quarter. Our_

_Almond Hair Oil franchise accelerated further. We remain on track to scale it to an_

_annualised revenue run rate of over 100 crore by FY ‘28, with a medium-term aspiration of_

_being a scaled challenger by building it into a 200-250 crore ARR franchise by FY ‘30.”_


_— Saugata Gupta, MD & CEO_


The company has demonstrated it can acquire small digital brands and significantly

increase their size and profit margins. This capability is becoming a core part of the

company’s growth strategy as they integrate new acquisitions like True Elements.


_“Over the years, our digital-first personal care portfolio has grown to about 1,150 crore, and_

_we have built serious holistic internal capabilities to scale up digital brands profitably._


_Beardo perfectly exemplifies this. Since we took over the brand under Marico’s leadership in_

_2020, the business has scaled by more than 6x and is expected to cross 400 crore this year._

_At the same time, profitability has improved by 1,900 bps. Our recent integration_

_experience with True Elements has also been very good. This is indeed strong proof of our_

_ability to deliver sustained and profitable growth across the digital brands we fully acquire_

_and scale up.”_


_— Saugata Gupta, MD & CEO_


The company is making a major push into the protein market by using four different

brands to target various consumer groups. They plan to repeat their success with oats

by launching more expensive, premium versions of their basic soya products.


_“If I look at protein, we see protein as a big bet, and we want to participate across different_

_categories, across different price points, and cater to different consumer segments._

_Therefore, holistically, we will operate and participate in protein through at least four_

_brands, which are Saffola, Cosmix, Plix, and True Elements. Our participation in protein is_

_aggressive and broad-based, while ensuring that we address multiple consumer segments._

_Coming to the mass end, we obviously participate in soya chunks. It is nearly 100 ARR, but_

_the question is—and you are absolutely right—that what we did with oats, we need to do_

_with soya chunks. Basically, how do we premiumize that segment while participating in the_

_mass end?”_


_— Saugata Gupta, MD & CEO_


Strategic price cuts on larger packs have stopped customers from switching to cheaper

alternatives. At the same time, a highly efficient supply chain has allowed the company

to reach rural markets faster and with less inventory risk than competitors.


_“I would say there are four drivers, besides the flawless execution we have done, which have_

_helped Parachute sustain its performance. Coming to this year specifically, what worked for_

_us was that we made selective price drops in loyalty packs, and that reversed the_

_downtrading we had been witnessing last year because of inflation. We passed on the_

_benefit in terms of adding volume in price-point packs, and the price adjustments were_

_higher in the mid and larger packs, which are loyalty packs. We made one price change._

_Secondly, because of {? Setu ?} and our rhythm, and because we believe in doing 3.5% sales_

_every day, our overall pipeline in trade and at the distributor level is thin. It is one of the_

_thinnest in the industry today. Previously, by the time the price drop was executed, it used_


_to take 4 weeks, or 8 weeks in rural areas. This has happened in record time. The third thing_

_that has also helped us, while it has now reached VAHO, is helping Parachute immensely_

_because we are going deep into rural markets with an increased number of stockists. We_

_have increased our stockists to nearly 1.8 times the earlier number.”_


_— Saugata Gupta, MD & CEO_

# Software Services

### Happiest Minds Technologies Ltd. | Small Cap


Happiest Minds is a digital transformation and IT services firm focusing on AI, cloud,

and cybersecurity. The company serves global clients across BFSI, EdTech, and

Healthcare sectors while transitioning toward an AI-first delivery model.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/40652-03-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


Management is targeting private equity firms to provide technology services to their

portfolio companies during modernisation phases. This strategy creates a reliable

pipeline of new clients who are actively seeking rapid operational improvements and

cost savings.


_“Our private equity ecosystem initiative provides another strategic avenue to engage PE_

_firms and their portfolio companies, supporting their technology modernisation,_

_operational transformation, and value creation priorities. Together, our GCC and PE_

_initiatives represent important growth avenues, enabling us to engage customers earlier in_

_their transformation journeys and progressively expand relationships across AI, engineering,_

_and enterprise technology services.”_


_— Joseph Anantharaju, Co-Chairman and CEO_


The company is winning major contracts by promising specific productivity gains

through the use of AI agents in software development. This allows them to underbid

competitors on fixed-price projects while still maintaining profitability through

automation.


_“The multi-million-dollar deal Joseph talked about has interesting AI productivity elements_

_as key outcomes that helped us secure the deal. We committed to a certain AI productivity_

_percentage as part of the transformation by agentifying the entire SDLC process by FY28._

_We talked about helps us gain higher AI productivity than using agents with foundation_

_models. This allowed us to make more aggressive bids than the competition on fixed-bid_

_deals. In Q2, we secured a deal with an Indian steel manufacturing company to build a_

_unified digital planning and execution platform that could transform the entire production_

_planning process from reactive to structured, transparent, and data-enabled.”_


_— Sridhar Mantha, CEO, Generative AI Business Services_


Stagnant growth in the US market is largely due to a downturn in the American

EdTech sector, where most of the company’s revenue in that vertical originates. To

counter this, management is shifting focus to EdTech opportunities in other regions

and splitting sales teams to target new logos more aggressively.


_“In terms of the ups and downs of the US geography, and what has positively impacted it_

_and what has been a little bit of a drag, I think our NN bookings have been positive in the_

_US geography. As you know, we divided our sales team into NN and EN a year and a half_

_ago, and I think, especially in the US, because we focus more on the US, we have seen good_

_traction there. I think what has been a little bit of a drag is that most of our EdTech_

_revenues, I would say 90% of our EdTech revenues, come from the US. The US EdTech_

_market has been going through its challenges. We have discussed this in the last couple of_

_calls. We are trying to address it in multiple ways by looking at the university segment, and_

_we are looking at our own offerings, where we have focused more on the Middle East, India,_

_and Southeast Asia.”_


_— Joseph Anantharaju, Co-Chairman and CEO_


The company uses specialised export-related borrowing that appears expensive on

paper but is actually offset by currency gains and interest income. This treasury

strategy results in a very low net cost for working capital, protecting the bottom line

from high domestic interest rates.


_“The interest rate cannot be viewed in isolation because it is linked to the forwards that we_

_take and the premiums we pay on them. The annualised premium on forwards is about_

_5.5%, but this works only if you are able to repay it on a particular date because of the_

_contractual commission conditions. Over the past 13 years, we have developed a structure_


_that has hopefully been streamlined, under which we borrow our working capital through_

_EPC or PCFC. That is the borrowing that you see. However, from an accounting_

_perspective, the benefit of the foreign exchange and the other income generated by the float_

_is reflected in the other-income line and the revenue or operating-margin line, whereas you_

_see only the interest cost as a single line item. Therefore, we are effectively looking at the_

_net cost as a whole. Simply put, the net cost will be in the range of about 0.5% in the worst_

_case, or 1% in the worst case, but in most cases it is actually beneficial to us.”_


_— Venkatraman Narayanan, Managing Director_


The company has successfully reduced its reliance on the struggling EdTech sector by

growing its share of business in Healthcare and BFSI. This diversification within its

largest accounts reduces the impact of sector-specific downturns on total revenue.


_“As you have rightly pointed out, the top-five composition has grown. We have had a little_

_bit of reshuffling in the top five, but not much. The top three customers earlier probably_

_included two from EdTech, but right now the composition is quite well distributed and_

_diversified. In the top three, we have one customer from the Healthcare space, another_

_customer from the BFSI space, and a third customer that continues to be in the EdTech_

_space. Rounding out the top five, there is one more customer in Healthcare and another one_

_in the EdTech space. As you can see, the EdTech customer is facing competition from a_

_customer in the Retail and CPG space. We have diversified well away from some of the risks_

_we had in EdTech and into verticals where there are more investments, growth, and_

_potential.”_


_— Sridhar Mantha, CEO, Generative AI Business Services_

# Tourism & Hospitality

### Thomas Cook (India) Ltd. | Small Cap


Thomas Cook (India) is a leading integrated travel and financial services provider

offering forex, corporate travel, and leisure holiday packages. The group also operates

a significant hospitality business through Sterling Holiday Resorts and global imaging

services via DEI.


<u>[[Concall]](https://files.tijoristack.ai/concall/transcript/303-03-Nov-2026.pdf)</u>


Management is balancing a strong recovery in domestic Indian travel against

geopolitical headwinds in the Middle East that have impacted specific subsidiaries.

Investors should note that while international exposure is still recovering, the direct

impact of the West Asia conflict is showing signs of moderation.


_“The quarter’s performance reflects 2 things. First, the strong recovery and growth in parts_

_of our India portfolio. Second, the impact of the conflict in West Asia, which is largely_

_limited to a few businesses with direct exposure to the region, namely Desert Adventures_
